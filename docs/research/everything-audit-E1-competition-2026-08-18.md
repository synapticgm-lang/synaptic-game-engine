# E1 — Current vs Competition Scorecard

**Research date:** 2026-08-18 (GMT+1). **Interpretation rule:** `PUBLICLY EVIDENCED` denotes a public page actually reviewed. `MARKETING CLAIM` is a competitor’s own positioning, not an independently tested outcome. `UNVERIFIED` means the audit did not locate sufficient public implementation detail and does not infer it. `Snapshot` means the supplied SynapticGM brief, not a build observation.

## How to Read This Scorecard

The SynapticGM column deliberately separates **claimed** capability from **evidence supplied in the Snapshot**. Because the template fields were empty and no URL/build/screenshots were supplied, every Snapshot item is a claimed current/building feature, not a passing behavior test. Each “Gap” therefore includes a proof obligation, not just an idea.

## Can We Win? — One-Page Verdict

**Yes, conditionally.** The market validates both sides of SynapticGM’s thesis: products openly wrestle with context limits, summarization/retrieval, memory prioritization, structured state, authored controls, and usage economics. AI Dungeon’s public explanation makes the central technical challenge unusually visible: it has to summarize, retrieve, trim, and—in some historic edit cases—ask the player to update the story summary. Friends & Fables similarly acknowledges that semantic long-term-memory search is less reliable than lore/entities and gives players controls to inspect/edit context. [1] [3]

The winning position is therefore not “we remember more” or “we use AI for RPGs.” It is **continuity you can inspect, correct, and trust without operating a memory-management console.** The necessary proof is a handful of camera-ready actions: correction persistence; source-aware kit truth; a player ignored hook that stays optional; receipt-to-state fairness; and a voice switch that cannot rewrite reality. If those work visibly at turn 10 and turn 100, SynapticGM has a claim that a user can repeat to another user.

The highest risk is false confidence from names alone. StateTx, SceneManifest, and IntentContract are valuable only if a player can find “what changed,” “why it changed,” and “how to correct it” in seconds. Until a live build and traces arrive, the moat is **UNVERIFIED**. The release plan should prefer a narrow, stable adult-web beta with camera-proof trust loops over broad engines, high-cost imagery, GPU work, or more architecture exploration.
