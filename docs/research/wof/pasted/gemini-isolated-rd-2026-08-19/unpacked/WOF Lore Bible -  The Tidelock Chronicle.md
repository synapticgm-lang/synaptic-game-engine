# WOF Lore Bible: The Tidelock Chronicle

**Status:** Active research baseline  
**Scope:** Original WOF fiction only  
**Design commitment:** Consequences are witnessed, named, and carried forward.

## Foundational premise

The world of **Veyr** was once held in balance by five living currents known as the Resonances: ember, brine, gale, root, and veil. Their concord did not create a perfect world. It made change comprehensible. Then the first Tidelock began: the currents fell out of sequence, and the world learned that places could remember futures that had not happened yet.

Each Tidelock has four phases—slack, rising, crest, and ebb. When a phase turns, routes wake or close, old promises become visible, and private grief acquires public weight. Expeditioners travel through these disruptions not as predestined heroes, but as people whose choices decide **which version of a place gets remembered**.

> **Core dramatic question:** What must a community relinquish to keep its future from being rewritten by its past?

## Tone, promises, and exclusions

WOF is a fantasy of weathered wonder, civic grief, intimate obligation, and dangerous repair. Its emotional palette balances salt-stained melancholy with purposeful hope. Beauty is tangible but never pristine: lantern soot, tideglass, old rope, lichen, ash, rain-polished brass, and hand-written testimony establish a world that has survived by tending what it cannot fully understand.

| Narrative promise | Player-facing expression | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wonder has a cost. | Magic leaves scars, debts, evidence, or altered relationships. | Frictionless power accumulation. |
| Places have agency. | A region reacts through conditions, routes, and threads. | Settings that exist only as combat backdrops. |
| No faction is morally blank. | Every group has a credible need and a concealed cost. | Purely evil institutions or arbitrary betrayal. |
| Memory is playable. | Past events become character memories with future triggers. | Lore dumps without mechanical consequence. |
| Repair is active. | Resolutions require trade-offs and lasting commitments. | Reset-button endings or consequence erasure. |

The tone should not borrow the courtly chivalric fantasy, standardized adventurer guild, or familiar medieval tavern-centred campaign loop. WOF instead favors **threshold fantasy**: ferry crossings, lighthouses, floodgates, memorial vaults, rope bridges, kiln districts, drifting gardens, and observatories submerged below an impossible tide.

## Cosmology: the Five Resonances

The Resonances are not elements and they are not deities. They are the world’s habits of becoming. Every craft, creature, region, and event may carry one or more resonances. Power comes from arranging resonances into a temporary agreement; failure occurs when someone forces an agreement without accepting the cost.

| Resonance | World habit | Human practice | Gift | Cost of misuse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ember** | Transformation through heat and attention. | Forging, cooking, burning, revision. | Reveals what can be remade. | It consumes distinctions and turns care into control. |
| **Brine** | Preservation through dissolution and return. | Navigation, mourning, fermentation, salt-work. | Carries messages across separation. | It blurs boundaries and draws old grief to the surface. |
| **Gale** | Motion through relation and release. | Song, flags, kites, migration, rumor. | Opens paths and changes perspective. | It scatters responsibility and promises. |
| **Root** | Continuance through binding and nourishment. | Gardening, weaving, construction, kinship. | Stabilizes what must endure. | It entangles living systems until they cannot adapt. |
| **Veil** | Meaning through absence, witness, and concealment. | Ritual, archive, medicine, silence. | Protects what cannot yet be understood. | It makes secrecy feel like safety and turns omission into harm. |

### Axioms of practice

Magic in WOF should be designed according to five axioms. The first is that a working must name the resonance it asks to change. The second is that every working leaves an observable sign. The third is that a working may transfer a burden but cannot eliminate it. The fourth is that witnesses alter the ethical shape of a working. The fifth is that no one can invoke a resonance indefinitely without becoming easier for that resonance to read.

These axioms support a **consequence-first magic loop**. A player chooses an intention, states or discovers the resonance involved, accepts a cost, and records the evidence as a memory, oath, relation change, route change, or regional condition. This approach aligns narrative texture with the kernel’s event-sourced state model.

## The Tidelock and progression loop

A campaign proceeds through short expeditions nested within a regional season. The party receives a visible pressure: an unstable route, a faction’s need, an overdue oath, a local thread, or a sudden phase change. They prepare with limited supplies and relationships, take an expedition-level action, and return with discoveries and consequences that update the chronicle.

| Loop stage | Fictional action | Kernel-facing state | Design test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Read the weather | Interpret the active phase and local pressure. | `Tidelock`, `StoryThread` | Can the player state what is urgent? |
| Commit | Choose a route, partner, and acceptable debt. | `Route`, `FactionRelation`, `Oath` | Is the trade-off legible before selection? |
| Make a working | Use a resonance to alter an obstacle or reveal a truth. | `Memory`, `WorldEvent` | Does power change future choices? |
| Return and witness | Record a cost, discovery, and changed relationship. | `Chronicle`, `Ledger` | Can a reader explain what changed and why? |
| Reorient | Re-evaluate regional paths after the Tidelock responds. | `Region`, `Thread`, `Route` | Does the next expedition emerge naturally? |

Progression is primarily **relational and cartographic**. Characters gain access to more difficult regions by resolving or transforming pressures in earlier places, earning or owing faction support, and retaining memories that let them confront related situations differently. Numerical growth should remain narrow and interpretable; the emotional growth is the greater reward.

## Regional progression path

The initial WOF research map is a five-region spiral. It supports a controlled test of gated opportunity without treating progression as linear conquest. Each region presents one dominant resonance, one secondary resonance, and an unresolved civic question.

| Tier | Region | Resonance signature | Surface conflict | Unlock condition | Lasting question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Hushmere** | Brine + Veil | The drowned observatory speaks through reed bells. | Starting region. | Who has the right to name a collective loss? |
| 2 | **Cinder Spine** | Ember + Gale | A fuel-less kiln forges objects from remembered disasters. | Discover an open route during rising or crest. | Can a tool be ethical if its origin is a wound? |
| 3 | **The Verdant Holdfast** | Root + Brine | A floating orchard is binding refugees to its roots for safety. | Resolve or fracture a tier-2 faction obligation. | When does protection become possession? |
| 4 | **The Wind Archive** | Gale + Veil | Testimonies migrate as weather and erase their original speakers. | Carry two contradictory memories into an ebb phase. | Is truth still truth when no one owns it? |
| 5 | **The Stillwater Crown** | All five, unstable | The original Tidelock is held in a drowned engine-city. | Enter with a kept oath, an open debt, and one unresolved thread. | Which costs should remain rather than be repaired? |

## Faction structure

Factions are engines of worldview rather than vendors of quests. Each one must be capable of giving help that feels necessary and asking for something that complicates the party’s self-image. A faction’s concealed cost should be discoverable through play, not merely revealed in exposition.

| Faction | Public face | Credo | Current need | Concealed cost | Typical pressure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Keepers of the Last Lantern** | Memorialists and field witnesses. | “What is witnessed may be mourned; what is hidden will return hungry.” | Guides for Hushmere’s observatory. | Their archives bind each witness to one unspeakable memory. | Testimony versus mercy. |
| **Ashwright Compact** | Artisans who forge with magical failures. | “A made thing deserves a maker who accepts its consequences.” | Brine-glass to quiet the eastern kiln. | Every tool retains an impulse from its originating disaster. | Utility versus consent. |
| **Morrowbind Orchard** | Stewards of floating gardens and evacuation routes. | “Nothing survives alone, so nothing should leave alone.” | Labor to secure drifting root-anchors. | Their safeguards make departure psychologically painful. | Care versus captivity. |
| **The Unmoored Choir** | Itinerant singers, couriers, and rumor-brokers. | “A truth that cannot travel becomes a weapon.” | Permission to release sealed testimonies. | Their songs dissolve individual authorship. | Circulation versus accountability. |
| **The Quiet Cartographers** | Surveyors of vanishing roads. | “A map is a promise with margins.” | Records from the Stillwater Crown. | They delete routes that lead to unbearable futures. | Orientation versus denial. |

## Character anchors

WOF characters are shaped by origin, resonance, memories, wounds, and oaths. Archetypes offer a stance toward consequence rather than a rigid combat role. A player may begin as a **Witness-binder**, **Kiln Runner**, **Rootless Gardener**, **Wind Courier**, or **Margin Seer**. Each archetype should have a stable action language, but none should imply moral alignment.

| Anchor | Mechanical expression | Fictional purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Origin region | A first relationship to place and local knowledge. | Gives each character a credible stake in the map. |
| Resonance profile | A narrow set of preferred workings. | Makes approaches distinct without creating element-caster stereotypes. |
| Memory | A named past event with a scar and trigger. | Lets history become a future affordance or vulnerability. |
| Oath | A voluntary, visible promise with a due turn. | Converts intention into a trackable source of pressure. |
| Wound and focus | Bounded capacity and aftermath. | Prevents consequence-free escalation. |

## Narrative grammar for new content

A WOF encounter should be written as a pressure with witnesses, not as a neutral obstacle. It needs a place that is changing, a faction or person who needs something understandable, a cost that has not yet been named, and at least two responses that each preserve a different value. The outcome should generate a concrete chronicle event.

```text
Place under pressure + witnessed need + unaccepted cost
→ commitment under a Tidelock phase
→ resonance working or relational bargain
→ named evidence in the chronicle
→ altered route, thread, memory, oath, or relation
```

The intended result is a setting where the world remembers what players did without requiring a hidden sprawling simulation. Any new lore must be recorded through the templates in this research directory before it becomes a fixture or kernel event.
