# Memorable Plate Style Guide

**Status:** **EVIDENCED** product direction supplied by the founder; the detailed rules and templates below are **ORIGINAL SynapticGM** production material.  
**Purpose:** Generate occasional book-plate illustrations for memorable moments without importing licensed-world names, studio styles, or character likenesses.

> **Core look:** An original storybook plate painted in expressive black or sepia ink, transparent watercolour washes, restrained opaque gouache accents, and visible cold-press paper grain. The image should feel authored, atmospheric, and specific to the current scene—not like a poster, screenshot, trading card, or generic concept-art splash.

## 1. Visual Grammar

| Dimension | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Medium | Use hand-drawn ink contours, broken dry-brush edges, transparent watercolour blooms, selective gouache highlights, and natural paper texture. Avoid photoreal rendering, glossy 3D materials, vector-flat shading, and hyper-finished cinematic concept art. |
| Composition | Show one decisive story beat. Use a clear foreground anchor, readable middle-ground action, and a simplified atmospheric distance. Leave intentional quiet space rather than filling every region. |
| Viewpoint | Default to an **adult player-character viewpoint**: eye level approximately 1.6–1.8 metres, adult hand proportions if hands are unavoidable, and adult-scale relation to doors, furniture, tools, and companions. Use a child viewpoint only when the supplied biography explicitly says the viewpoint character is a child. |
| Character handling | Prefer rear three-quarter, side, obscured, or small-in-frame figures. When the player biography supplies appearance, preserve those anchors. Do not invent age, ethnicity, disability, body type, costume rank, insignia, or sexualized details. |
| Faces | Faces may be simplified by ink economy or turned away. Do not generate close portrait plates unless the scene itself is a portrait moment and the biography supports it. No celebrity likenesses. |
| Light | Choose one dominant light event: window rain-light, lantern amber, dawn haze, reflected fire, moonlit snow, undersea glow, terminal phosphor, or crystal refraction. Let washes carry atmosphere; do not add neon merely to make the image dramatic. |
| Palette | Use two dominant colour families plus one small accent. Preserve paper white. Dark scenes should still retain wash separation and readable silhouettes rather than collapsing to black. |
| Detail hierarchy | Highest detail belongs to the story-changing object or gesture, second to the immediate environment, and least to distant architecture. Do not render every inventory item. |
| Text | No readable text, numbers, labels, sigils, UI, subtitles, speech balloons, captions, logos, signatures, or watermarks inside the image. A book, screen, sign, or letter may appear only with abstract illegible marks. |
| World originality | Use generic folklore, natural history, urban life, speculative engineering, and public-domain architectural traditions. Never request another creator’s, studio’s, game’s, series’, or franchise’s style. |
| Violence | Depict consequence and tension without fetishized injury. Use torn cloth, smoke, dropped objects, distance, or posture rather than exposed wounds. No graphic gore. |
| Kid Mode | Remove alcohol focus, gambling emphasis, sexual framing, graphic injury, body horror, and sadistic imagery. Preserve stakes through weather, separation, broken tools, threatening shadows, blocked paths, and urgent choices. |

## 2. Prompt Assembly Order

A production prompt should name the **story fact first**, then the visible composition, viewpoint, medium, light, palette, and exclusions. This keeps the generator anchored to what happened rather than to decorative genre noise.

| Prompt block | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Story fact | Who is present, where the moment occurs, what physically changed, and the one object or action that matters. |
| Composition | Shot distance, camera height, foreground anchor, middle-ground action, and background simplification. |
| Continuity anchors | Biography-backed appearance, clothing, carried object, companion count, weather, time, interior/exterior, and location architecture. |
| Medium | Original ink-and-watercolour book plate, restrained gouache, cold-press paper, imperfect line weight. |
| Light and palette | One dominant source, two colour families, one accent. |
| Safety and exclusions | No readable text, UI, logos, watermarks, branded designs, sexualization, graphic gore, or licensed-world references. |

### Master Suffix

Append this suffix to any template after replacing brackets:

> Original SynapticGM storybook plate; expressive ink line, transparent watercolour washes, restrained gouache highlights, visible cold-press paper grain, selective detail, preserved paper white, coherent adult-scale perspective unless the biography explicitly identifies a child viewpoint; no readable text, no numbers, no UI, no logos, no signatures, no watermarks, no brand marks, no copyrighted-world references, no imitation of a named artist or studio, no sexualized framing, no graphic gore.

## 3. Continuity Checklist

| Question | If unknown |
| --- | --- |
| Is the viewpoint character an adult or a biography-confirmed child? | Use adult viewpoint; do not infer a child from a school-like setting alone. |
| Which named companions are physically present? | Show no companion rather than inventing one. |
| What is in the character’s hands? | Show empty or obscured hands unless the snapshot confirms an object. |
| Is the scene indoors or outdoors? | Keep architecture ambiguous and avoid a horizon if the snapshot does not settle it. |
| What time and weather are active? | Use neutral diffuse light; do not invent rain, sunset, snow, or moonlight. |
| Which item or environmental change made the moment memorable? | Emphasize only the confirmed change. |
| Does a title, rank, faction, or heraldry exist in state? | Omit insignia and ceremonial markers unless confirmed. |

## 4. Twenty Original Scene-Prompt Templates

### Template 01 — Threshold Decision

**Use when:** The player chooses whether to cross a dangerous or meaningful boundary.

> Show [viewpoint character] paused at [threshold], with [confirmed object or empty hand] nearest the foreground and [known destination feature] beyond. Adult eye-level view from just behind one shoulder; the threshold line cuts diagonally across the page while the far side dissolves into simplified wash. Light comes from [confirmed source], using [palette family one] and [palette family two] with a small [accent] note. Emphasize hesitation through posture and distance, not facial melodrama. Append the Master Suffix.

### Template 02 — First Relic Recognition

**Use when:** An identified object becomes the centre of a bound-relic pattern.

> A close but not macro book plate of [identified relic] resting on [confirmed surface] while [viewpoint character] reaches toward it without yet grasping it. The object carries one visible material tell—[confirmed tell]—and the cost is implied by [non-graphic environmental consequence]. Keep the character secondary and partly cropped. Adult hand scale unless the biography explicitly establishes a child. Use quiet surrounding washes so the relic is the sole high-detail anchor. Append the Master Suffix.

### Template 03 — Companion Reappears

**Use when:** A known, snapshot-present companion returns to view.

> Frame [confirmed companion] emerging from [doorway, mist, stair, or crowd gap actually present] while [viewpoint character] remains a small foreground anchor. Show relief through spacing and lowered shoulders, not an invented embrace. Preserve the confirmed companion count and appearance; include no unnamed followers. Use [light source] to separate the returning silhouette from [location material]. Append the Master Suffix.

### Template 04 — Empty-Hand Realisation

**Use when:** The player discovers an expected item is absent or spent.

> Show [viewpoint character] looking down at an open, empty hand above [confirmed surface], with the place where [missing item] should have been indicated only by a pale unpainted shape or displaced dust. Keep all other inventory out of frame. Adult-scale hand and sleeve based on biography; no new jewellery or insignia. Use a narrow palette and generous paper white to make absence the subject. Append the Master Suffix.

### Template 05 — Last Light in the Shelter

**Use when:** A safe interior becomes uncertain because its light is failing.

> Interior book plate of [confirmed shelter] as [confirmed lamp, fire, terminal, or crystal] dims. The player is shown from behind at adult eye level, surrounded by only the people and objects confirmed present. Let the corners pool into cool wash while a final [warm or coloured] accent touches the immediate decision point. No exterior weather unless visible in state. Append the Master Suffix.

### Template 06 — Weather Turns

**Use when:** The weather genuinely changes in the snapshot.

> Wide environmental plate of [location] at the exact moment [old weather] gives way to [new weather]. Place [viewpoint character] small and adult-scaled beside [confirmed landmark], with fabric, foliage, dust, or water reacting naturally. Split the washes so the incoming weather occupies one side and the retreating condition the other. Do not add lightning, ruins, or creatures unless confirmed. Append the Master Suffix.

### Template 07 — The Quiet Crowd Parts

**Use when:** A confirmed crowd reacts and creates a path.

> Street- or hall-level plate showing the confirmed crowd of [approximate snapshot size] opening a narrow passage toward [known speaker, object, or exit]. Keep faces simplified and individually unimportant; no extra factions, uniforms, or weapons. The player’s adult viewpoint sits at the mouth of the passage, making the empty lane the compositional focal shape. Append the Master Suffix.

### Template 08 — Exit Used

**Use when:** The player leaves through a specific confirmed exit.

> Show the view back through [used exit] after [viewpoint character] has crossed it: the opened door, lifted hatch, broken seal, or parted curtain remains foreground evidence, while the prior location recedes beyond. The player may appear only as a partial adult-scale shadow on the new side. Preserve time, weather, and indoor/outdoor relation exactly. Append the Master Suffix.

### Template 09 — Book Becomes Skill

**Use when:** A readable/codex item leads to a committed named skill.

> A table-level plate of [confirmed book or manual] open to abstract, illegible diagrams while a single visual metaphor for [committed skill] appears in the materials around it—[heat pattern, aligned tools, moving air, geometric reflection]—without floating UI or readable words. The player’s adult hands hold the page edges only if confirmed free. Keep the named skill out of the image itself. Append the Master Suffix.

### Template 10 — Offer After the Deed

**Use when:** The system offers a skill because of what the player just did.

> Depict the physical aftermath of [confirmed deed] in [location], with one subtle environmental echo of the offered capability. Do not show a menu, holographic prompt, or readable system text. The player stands at adult scale in the composition, visibly able to accept or refuse through a paused gesture rather than a literal button. Use one small accent colour for the offer’s presence. Append the Master Suffix.

### Template 11 — Refusal Holds

**Use when:** A player declines an offer and the refusal persists.

> Quiet plate after [declined offer]: [viewpoint character] turns away from [object, path, or environmental lure] while the physical world remains unchanged and available consequences stay visible. The image should communicate agency through direction and spacing, not moral judgment. No vanishing menu, shattered magic, or angry entity unless confirmed. Adult viewpoint by default. Append the Master Suffix.

### Template 12 — Map Becomes Legible

**Use when:** A confirmed location relationship is discovered.

> Overhead-leaning adult viewpoint of [confirmed map, floor plan, or terrain] beside [one location anchor], with inked but unreadable marks resolving into a clearer route through composition rather than text. A finger, compass, key, or light beam may indicate the route only if present in snapshot. Do not invent place names, borders, or heraldry. Append the Master Suffix.

### Template 13 — Repair Confirmed

**Use when:** The product corrects an invented or ungrounded detail and restores the supported scene.

> Show the corrected physical scene only: [supported object/person/speaker/location] occupies the clear focal position while the rejected invention is absent, leaving no ghost, crossed-out symbol, or apology text. Compose the plate as a calm return to continuity with stable horizon, balanced wash, and one familiar material anchor from the location. Adult viewpoint unless biography says child. Append the Master Suffix.

### Template 14 — Rumour Takes Shape

**Use when:** The player hears a rumour but has not accepted a quest.

> Social interior or street plate of [confirmed speaker] leaning toward the player while [rumoured place/object/event] appears only as an ambiguous wash vignette in smoke, reflection, or background negative space. The player remains adult-scaled and no agreement gesture is shown. Include only present listeners. No quest marker, contract, title, or guaranteed truth. Append the Master Suffix.

### Template 15 — Soft Offer, Door Still Open

**Use when:** A possible quest is offered without commitment.

> [Confirmed speaker or circumstance] presents a practical opening—[map edge, unlocked door, set-down tool, offered seat]—between the adult-viewpoint player and [known objective direction]. Keep the composition unresolved, with equal visual weight on engagement and withdrawal routes. No handshake, accepted token, celebratory light, or menu icon. Append the Master Suffix.

### Template 16 — Accepted Work

**Use when:** A quest is explicitly accepted.

> Show the exact physical act that confirms acceptance: [taking a token, repeating terms, receiving a map, opening a gate], using only objects and people present in snapshot. The player’s adult hand or posture should make commitment unambiguous without readable contracts or interface markers. Keep the objective itself distant or partial so the plate records provenance, not completion. Append the Master Suffix.

### Template 17 — Indoor to Outdoor Shift

**Use when:** The scene genuinely crosses from inside to outside.

> Compose [interior material] as a dark framing edge around a newly visible [exterior condition], with the adult-viewpoint player mid-step across the boundary. Preserve the confirmed exit and weather. Let paper white or pale wash open toward the exterior while interior detail compresses behind. Do not add a landscape landmark that state has not established. Append the Master Suffix.

### Template 18 — Tension Drops Without Vanishing

**Use when:** Immediate danger eases but consequences remain.

> After [confirmed threat or pressure] recedes, show [viewpoint character] lowering [confirmed tool or empty hands] while one environmental trace—smoke, broken latch, retreating ripples, scattered papers—proves the event occurred. Use more open paper and softer edges, but retain the unresolved consequence at a corner. No sudden celebration or invented safety. Append the Master Suffix.

### Template 19 — Named Speaker in Place

**Use when:** A known speaker delivers a pivotal line while physically present.

> Medium-wide plate of [confirmed named speaker] positioned in [their confirmed location] addressing the adult-viewpoint player. The speaker’s mouth need not be sharply rendered; gesture and spatial relation carry the exchange. Include no speech balloon or readable text. Preserve who else is present and keep the location itself silent—walls, signs, or objects must not appear to speak. Append the Master Suffix.

### Template 20 — After the Choice

**Use when:** A turn creates one durable state change.

> Depict [single confirmed changed fact] through its physical consequence in [location]: [object moved, door open, companion present, light changed, weather altered, item consumed]. Keep unchanged facts visually stable. The player appears at adult scale unless the biography explicitly establishes a child. Use one crisp ink anchor for the change and softer washes for everything that merely continues. Append the Master Suffix.

## 5. Reject Conditions

A generated plate fails if it invents a companion, places an absent item in hand, adds an unsupported title or uniform, turns architecture into a speaker, changes time or weather, introduces readable text, resembles a named franchise, sexualizes a figure, shows graphic gore, or shifts to a child viewpoint without biography support. A failed plate should be regenerated from corrected facts; do not patch continuity errors with captions.
