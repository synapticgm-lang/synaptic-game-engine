# Draft A–B — Gap Analysis and Recommended Pipeline

> This is an intermediate synthesis. The final package will renumber citations and rewrite for a single coherent document.

## A) Gap analysis vs industry comic generators — technique shapes only

SynapticGM should not compete with offline authoring suites on “generate a whole comic from one prompt.” A live game has a stricter problem: it must preserve a committed story state, return control immediately, and keep spend bounded. Public creator workflows show useful **technique shapes**—format-specific layout banks, shot contracts, local panel repair, reference conditioning, mobile preview, and layered compositing—but their autonomous script expansion and all-in-one page generation are unsafe defaults for live play.

| Public technique family | Effect bought | Public method | Cost/latency implication | Consistency failure mode | What SynapticGM should steal | What SynapticGM must never copy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vertical-scroll creator tools | Mobile readability, deliberate pacing, and dramatic pauses | Mostly top-to-bottom panel order, adjustable inter-panel space, long-canvas preview, mobile viewport preview, and later segmentation for publication [Clip Studio] | DOM layout is near-zero cost; every extra generated panel remains a separate paid job | Rhythm breaks if a panel is too tall, a gutter is arbitrary, or dialogue density exceeds a phone viewport | A responsive DOM stack, three semantic gutter tokens, and viewport fixtures; keep panels independent for repair | One baked tall image; universal pixel gutter “standards”; live lettering in art; creator-platform upload dimensions treated as product requirements |
| Layout-first AI comic tools | Rapid concept-to-page flow and local iteration | Modular story/planner/render stages; public tools claim format selection, character setup, script breakdown, panel generation, local regeneration, and editable lettering [AI Comic Factory; Anifusion] | Planning is cheaper than rendering; local repair avoids regenerating correct panels; LoRA/reference features add compute and storage | Autonomous breakdown invents beats; identity and kit drift; whole-page generation couples failures | Separate accepted BeatSpec, pointer card, PanelSpec, hosted render, and overlay; repair a single panel | Vendor asset libraries, protected style presets, planner authority over the ledger, or “unlimited” regeneration economics |
| Reference-image workflows | Better recurring-subject and visual-theme similarity | Subject/reference image plus text; separate style reference; adjustable conditioning strength; seed for experiments; semantic weighting [Midjourney official docs] | Reference conditioning can increase compute and disable some editing modes; seeds are almost free but weak | Fine details, clothing, and multi-character binding drift; high strength can distort; seeds do not preserve identity | Rights-cleared character/place references where the endpoint supports them; original technique boards; seed logging; provider-neutral priority sections | Treating seed as identity lock; uploading unlicensed art; protected style imitation; exposing foreign provider flags in product prompts |
| Storyboard/script-to-shot systems | Inspectable conversion from text to shots, camera control, and local revision | Script upload → shot list → image per shot → refinement; camera families and local frame edits [Storyboarder.ai] | Shot metadata is cheap; local repair controls re-spend; 3D camera features are product-specific | Shot planner adds uncommitted actions or “improves” the story; camera novelty obscures required facts | A deterministic shot contract: role, roster, place, kit, action boundary, camera family, and text-safe zone | Assuming 3D reframing exists in Klein/Flux; letting camera planning determine outcome or damage |
| Academic reading-order and closure research | Predictable reading path and meaningful panel transitions | Readers follow conventional order until separation, overlap, blockage, or irregularity redirects them; closure is the inference across gutters [Cohn & Campbell; Iyyer et al.] | Conservative layout logic is effectively free; complex interpretation or vision checking is not | Decorative layouts scramble order; generated panels imply a different transition; image-only interpretation hallucinates | Monotonic LTR/top-to-bottom templates; accepted ledger encodes the state change; panels show approved boundary states | Overlaps, blockers, and fancy insets in P0/P1; vision-model interpretation as story truth |
| Modular visual-narrative generators | Reusable sequence, composition, transition, and render layers | Narrative roles map to panels; composition and transition layers refine a sequence before rendering [Chen & Jhala] | Deterministic rule layers are cheap and testable | A generative action layer can choose new content not present in the story | Map accepted beats to establishing/action/reaction/reveal/aftermath/dialogue/transition roles; role selects layout, never content | Importing sample assets or allowing a refinement layer to create actions, entities, or outcomes |
| Visual-novel and VTT layering | Near-instant visual storytelling from reusable assets | Background/foreground separation, tagged portraits or sprite attributes, UI text layers, positioned tiles, z-order, opacity, scene state, and preloading [Ren’Py; Foundry VTT] | Near-zero model spend and low latency after assets are cached | Stale layers preserve the wrong character, weapon, wound, or place; combinations conflict | Deterministic Free fallback; atomic scene replacement; equipped-slot groups; explicit z-order and cache keys | Treating visual state as canonical, speculative generation during preload, or showing absent props because an asset exists |

### Verdict

**Behind:** SynapticGM lacks a validated layout bank, per-panel repair UX, reference-conditioned character/place workflow, deterministic fallback library, mobile pacing tests, and tier-aware economic policy. Its current text-only visual consistency block cannot reliably bind multiple recurring characters, equipment, and place across changing shots. The unreliable GM `<panel>` fallback also couples story wording to presentation markup without a guaranteed planning contract.

**Ahead:** SynapticGM is structurally ahead where a live game most needs to be: text/ledger truth, separate HTML/SVG lettering, non-blocking post-commit jobs, explicit Kid Mode pre-spend policy, hard panel budgets, and a hosted player path. Academic evidence that neither image nor text alone solves comic narrative and that gutters require reader inference reinforces—not weakens—the choice to keep accepted text authoritative.

**Highest-leverage gaps for hosted Klein/Flux:** First, replace Director dependence with deterministic BeatSpec/layout templates. Second, pass rights-cleared reference images only through capability-checked model adapters. Third, add immutable overlay receipts and local panel repair. Fourth, add a deterministic portrait/background composite when generation is skipped or fails. Fifth, measure eligible-turn rate and paid retry rate before widening panel frequency.

## B) Recommended pipeline — end to end

### B.1 Player-facing contract

The player selects **Classic**, **Comic**, or an existing compatible presentation. Comic settings choose paged or webtoon layout and a frequency policy; they do not promise an image on every turn. When an accepted beat is eligible, the UI immediately renders canonical text and an optional pending comic tile. Input remains available. A completed image replaces only its pending tile if the job still matches the current beat revision. A failed, stale, unsafe, or contradictory image collapses to text/comic chrome or a deterministic fallback without stopping play.

The repair control says what it does: **Retry art** repeats the same approved PanelSpec; **Simplify art** reduces roster/camera complexity; **Use portrait scene** switches to deterministic composition; **Hide art** keeps the story and overlay. No repair changes accepted dialogue, outcomes, injuries, inventory, relationships, or location.

### B.2 System pipeline

| Stage | Input | Deterministic work | Optional model work | Hard output/guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Commit story truth | Accepted GM/player turn and ledger delta | Commit text, speaker IDs, entity IDs, place ID, equipped slots, safety class, and revision ID | None | Play continues even if all later stages fail |
| 2. Eligibility gate | Committed beat metadata, tier, settings, capacity, Kid result, recent-art history | Apply skip rules, kill switch, panel budget, daily/session caps, and duplicate suppression | None | Ineligible beats spend zero |
| 3. Select panel-plan source | Validated GM tags or deterministic BeatSpec | Use strict precedence and reject malformed/inventive tags | Optional planner only after P1 evidence, never as critic | A bounded PanelPlan with one to three approved panels |
| 4. Select pointer card | Beat roles, layout mode, panel count | Choose a code-selectable template and semantic gutter token | None | Explicit reading order and one permitted text anchor per overlay |
| 5. Compile PanelSpec | Ledger entities/place/kit plus pointer card | Bind roster, action boundary, camera family, reference IDs, aspect ratio, text-safe zone, and negative list | None | Schema-valid, immutable art contract; no prose invention |
| 6. Kid and safety preflight | PanelSpec and accepted text | Rewrite framing or skip before generation; remove sexualised, gore-glamour, or otherwise unsafe framing | Existing safety service if already part of product, not a continuity critic | Safe PanelSpec or no-spend fallback |
| 7. Compile pure-art prompt | PanelSpec, visualConsistency, original style token bundle | Produce ordered visual sections and `PURE_ART_DIRECTIVE`; adapter maps supported parameters | None | Explicitly no dialogue, captions, SFX text, logos, UI, or watermarks |
| 8. Debit and enqueue | Model tier, request ID, capacity record | Atomic idempotent debit, then post-commit image job | Hosted OpenRouter image endpoint | No double debit; text/input already live |
| 9. Generate and validate transport | Prompt, optional references, seed, aspect ratio, output format | Capability allowlist, timeout, retry class, provenance, and job/revision checks | Klein 4B or Pro | One image per request; late/stale results never attach |
| 10. Present art plus overlay | Accepted text and valid current image | Render ComicGrid plus SpeechBubble/NarrativeText/ActionOverlay; bind speaker/utterance IDs | None | Overlay text is canonical and accessible; image is decorative evidence only |
| 11. Repair or degrade | Failure reason, panel complexity, remaining capacity | Retry once if transient; simplify or deterministic fallback if semantic; collapse if exhausted | Optional paid retry according to tier | No hidden loop, no new story fact, and spend remains capped |
| 12. Reuse and recap | Validated panels/Memorable plates plus provenance | Cache by entity/place/revision/style family and reuse only while semantically valid | Optional recap generation later | Reuse cannot revive corrected or superseded art |

### B.3 Panel-plan source precedence

| Situation | Source | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| A committed GM response contains well-formed `<panel>` tags that parse, reference only accepted roster/place/kit/action, fit `panelBudget`, and pass safety | **Validated GM tags** | Tags supply candidate beat roles, narrative overlay, and pure visual intent; canonical fields are still rebound from the ledger. Unknown entities, outcomes, wounds, or over-budget panels invalidate the art plan, not the turn. |
| Tags are absent, malformed, over budget, unsafe, contradictory, or too verbose | **Deterministic BeatSpec + layout bank** | Select one salient committed beat and map it to a known one-, two-, or three-panel role sequence. This is P0/P1 default. |
| A future optional planner is enabled for Mid/High after strip gates pass | **Light planner constrained by accepted facts** | It may choose among approved beat roles, pointer cards, and camera families. It may not add nouns, actions, outcomes, or text; failure falls back to templates. |
| No eligible visual beat or capacity | **No generated PanelPlan** | Show Classic/comic chrome, Memorable plate reuse, or deterministic portrait/background composition. |

### B.4 Director decision

**Keep Director hard-disabled for P0.** It is not required to ship the thin wedge, and re-enabling it would add latency, spend, nondeterminism, and a second failure surface before the image path is trustworthy. Replace the P0 planning function with deterministic BeatSpec/layout templates. In P1, an optional **light planner** may be tested for Mid/High only, behind an experiment flag and strict schema/ledger binding. It is a presentation planner, not a Continuity-Warden or truth critic; it can choose a template and camera family but cannot create content. If it does not beat deterministic templates on adherence, repair rate, latency, and player preference, it remains off.

### B.5 Classic and Memorable reuse

Classic and Memorable should share **presentation chrome**, not ComicGrid behavior. A Memorable opener or rare plate can receive the same accessible caption/bubble/receipt components, border treatment, speaker binding, pending/error state, and zoom gesture used by comic mode. Classic remains prose-first and never becomes a multi-panel page. The shared chrome reduces duplicate UX work and makes the P0 wedge visible to more users without violating the rule that `classic-book` never uses `ComicGrid`.

### B.6 Failure semantics

Transport errors, timeouts, moderation blocks, malformed image responses, or provider outages show a non-blocking failed tile with a deterministic fallback. Semantic contradictions—extra NPC, wrong weapon, wrong place, impossible wound, or unsafe Kid framing—must be rejected or hidden when detected by known metadata/user repair, but SynapticGM must not introduce an LLM image critic in the critical path. The P0 strategy is prevention by constrained prompt/reference inputs, conservative camera/roster limits, explicit user repair, and fixture sampling by humans during rollout.
