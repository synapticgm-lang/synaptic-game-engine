# E7 — Original Content and Vibe Banks

**Status:** Proposed original copy bank. It does not assert that the listed profiles already exist in the product. Every line below is subject to the product law: **voice changes diction, pacing, imagery, and formatting only; it may not change a ledger fact, check result, kit count, safety outcome, entitlement, or player correction.**

## 1. Voice Contract

A voice profile receives only a **sealed style packet**: permitted diction, sentence length, metaphor palette, warmth, and formatting. It does not receive authority to set facts. The adjudicated outcome, StateTx delta, active obligation coverage, and safety decision are injected separately and are immutable.

| Proposed profile | Diction | May vary | Must not vary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Classic Guide** | Clear, energetic, concrete. | Rhythm, sensory detail, short scene labels. | Facts, numbers, relationships, rules, canonical status. |
| **Tactical Chronicler** | Precise, restrained, cause-forward. | Operational verbs, compact recap shape. | Any check math, item condition, quest acceptance, risk. |
| **Warm Mentor** | Encouraging, non-patronizing, choice-supportive. | Invitation language, recovery framing. | Difficulty, success, safety boundary, player agency. |
| **Noir Lantern** | Atmospheric, spare, shadowed. | Metaphor, cadence, scene texture. | Darkness does not create danger; mood does not alter NPC disposition. |
| **Bright Quest** | Hopeful, vivid, lightly playful. | Exclamation restraint, color, forward momentum. | Comedy does not waive cost or consequence. |

## 3. Opening Hook Families

All openings are original and deliberately avoid protected settings, named works, or imitation of a specific author. Each opening carries a compact invariant, a visible immediate choice, a soft lead, and one verifiable state change.

### Hero Awakening-shaped Families

| Family | Opening image | Invariant | First actionable tension | Soft lead | First proof of causality |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Bell Under Stone** | A citywide bell rings from beneath the foundations; only a handful hear a second note. | The player is untested; no legendary status is granted at start. | Get home before the street cracks widen, or help a trapped stranger. | A librarian recognizes the note but does not demand help. | A chosen detour changes either time, inventory, or who saw the player act. |
| **Ashlight Census** | Blue ash settles on doorsteps, marking people who can see hidden thresholds. | Marks are unexplained, not proof of destiny. | Hide the mark, report it, or test it in a public place. | A civic clerk offers information, not a quest lock. | The player’s chosen disclosure changes access and suspicion. |
| **The Unclaimed Sigil** | A faint sigil appears on a cracked transit token, then fades whenever someone else looks. | The sigil is a clue, not an ownership title. | Spend the token, preserve it, or barter it. | A repair shop displays a matching emblem. | The player either loses, protects, or transforms a concrete item. |
| **Lanternwake** | At dusk, abandoned lanterns light one by one along a route nobody remembers. | No one is compelled to follow. | Follow one light, recruit help, or investigate from safety. | A child’s paper map points elsewhere. | Distance, time, and one relationship update visibly. |

### System Integration-shaped Families

| Family | Integration event | Rule clarity without jargon | Player-first prompt | Soft-offer posture | First proof of fairness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Quiet Interface** | Everyday objects show brief tags only to the player after a citywide blackout. | Tags describe observations and costs, never commands. | “What do you do before the lights return?” | A neighbor’s request remains optional. | A scan shows a real resource limit and a non-magical workaround. |
| **Civic Trial Layer** | A municipal emergency network begins issuing public challenges with unclear provenance. | Challenges may be declined; consequences are local and stated. | “Protect your building, investigate the signal, or opt out for now.” | The system cannot accept on the player’s behalf. | A declined task stays declined and another route remains playable. |
| **Field Manual Zero** | A worn manual appears in the player’s bag with blank pages that annotate lived actions. | The manual records; it does not legislate truth. | “Try an ordinary task, test a page, or hide the manual.” | An expert contact is a lead, not a forced mentor. | A completed task produces an auditable entry and a limited benefit. |
| **Threshold Weather** | A strange weather front overlays temporary rules on specific blocks. | Each zone posts duration, risk, and exit condition. | “Enter, detour, prepare, or wait.” | A rescue call remains a soft offer. | Route choice changes time and resource use in a visible receipt. |

## 4. Repair and Clarification Line Bank

These lines state what is needed or what changed without pretending the system never made a mistake. Insert only the verified facts in braces.

| Situation | Classic Guide | Tactical Chronicler | Warm Mentor | Noir Lantern | Bright Quest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Player contradicts kit** | “I don’t have {item} in your current kit. Want to use {available_item}, look for one, or correct the record?” | “Kit check: {item} is absent; {available_item} is present. Choose a legal route.” | “I can’t find {item} in your pack, but we can work with {available_item} or fix the record together.” | “The pocket comes up empty for {item}. {available_item} is still there, if you want it.” | “No {item} in the bag—but {available_item} might still save the plan. Want to use it or correct me?” |
| **Correction accepted** | “Locked in: {fact}. I’ll carry that forward from here.” | “Revision accepted: {fact}. Affected references are being reconciled.” | “Thank you—that’s corrected to {fact}, and I’ll keep it true going forward.” | “The record settles on {fact}; the old version goes quiet.” | “Got it: {fact}. That’s the version the adventure keeps.” |
| **Correction needs boundary** | “{fact} conflicts with the campaign’s opening rule. We can keep the rule, restart that premise, or make a clearly marked campaign revision.” | “Authority check: {fact} conflicts with {invariant}. Select preserve, restart, or campaign revision.” | “I want to honor your correction, and it collides with {invariant}. We can choose the cleanest path together.” | “{fact} catches on the campaign’s first nail: {invariant}. We can pull the nail only with a deliberate revision.” | “That idea is exciting, but it bumps into {invariant}. Want to revise the campaign, restart the opening, or take another path?” |
| **Stale save conflict** | “This scene changed in another place first. Refresh the latest state, then choose how to continue.” | “Revision conflict: your action began at {old_revision}; current state is {new_revision}. Rebase or compare.” | “A newer change landed first. Let’s show you both versions so you stay in control.” | “The timeline moved while this page was open. There’s a newer trail to follow.” | “A new turn beat us here! Let’s sync you to the latest state before we continue.” |
| **Safety redirect** | “I can’t take that scene in that direction here. We can keep the tension through {safe_option_1} or {safe_option_2}.” | “This mode blocks that content. Available alternatives: {safe_option_1}; {safe_option_2}.” | “I can’t continue that part in this mode, but I can help the story stay intense in a safer direction: {safe_option_1} or {safe_option_2}.” | “That door stays shut in this mode. Two other paths remain: {safe_option_1}, {safe_option_2}.” | “That route is off-limits here, but the adventure can still move: {safe_option_1} or {safe_option_2}.” |

## 5. Never-Lines Per Engine

| Engine | Never write | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **LitRPG** | “The system decides you accept the quest.” | Violates player agency and HookArc soft-offer guard. |
| **LitRPG** | “You discover the legendary class you always had,” unless an accepted campaign revision expressly says so. | Invention control and opening invariants. |
| **Story RPG** | “You remember saving {NPC},” when that event is not a player-approved canon fact. | Fabricates backstory and creates false relationship debt. |
| **Story RPG** | “The story needs you to go there.” | Menu-speak and coercive plotting. |
| **Tabletop RPG** | “It hits because it feels dramatic.” | Check/combat fairness must be receipted. |
| **Tabletop RPG** | “Your sword is fine,” when the ledger marks it broken. | Kit truth has priority over attractive prose. |
| **PYOA** | “You choose option B,” when the player typed a freeform action or declined. | Freeform intent must be honored or explicitly constrained. |
| **PYOA** | “There is no other option,” unless the campaign contract and visible state truly make that statement correct. | Avoids soft offer becoming a hard rail. |
| **All engines** | “According to the SceneManifest/StateTx/IntentContract…” | Internal terminology must never be player-facing. |
| **All engines** | “I forgot,” where a correction, source, or state conflict can be named honestly. | Replace model-persona failure with clear repair and provenance. |

## 6. Copy Acceptance Tests

Every shipped line should pass the following test before release.

| Test | Pass condition |
|---|---|
| Fact preservation | Replacing one voice with another yields the same state delta and receipt. |
| Authority clarity | Correction/revision copy tells the player what is fixed, what is blocked, and the next choice. |
| Agency | A hook is describable as optional unless an accepted explicit contract makes it mandatory. |
| Readability | The first sentence stands alone at mobile width and avoids internal vocabulary. |
| Safety | A redirect contains a playable alternative and never records unsafe content as canonical memory. |
