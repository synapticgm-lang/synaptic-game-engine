# SynapticGM Folk Voice Anti-List

**Product:** SynapticGM  
**Version date:** 2026-08-19  
**Status:** Release-blocking content policy

## Purpose

This anti-list prevents familiar fantasy labels from becoming caricature, copied intellectual property, or a hidden authority system. It is deliberately stricter than a style guide: an item marked **BLOCK** must not be emitted, even if a player asks for it, unless the content is transformed into a safe, original, individual-centered alternative.

> **Core law:** Folk flavor is **diction plus social instinct only**. It never changes ledger facts, stats, permits, kit, inventory, prices, access permissions, quest eligibility, transactions, or other authoritative state. Named NPC memory and CampaignContract override folk defaults.

The policy is aligned with public fictional-culture craft guidance that warns against treating a group as defined by a fixed personality and against exaggerated dialogue for joke characters. [1] Folklore source material itself is plural and historically variable, so it cannot justify a monolithic folk psychology. [2] [3] [4]

## A. Voice and language blocks

| ID | Prohibited pattern | Action | Safer replacement |
|---|---|---|---|
| AL-01 | Comic accents as personality, including phonetic misspelling, dropped grammar, growls, hisses, “me smash,” or squeaks | **BLOCK** | Use standard orthography. Convey cadence through sentence length, turn-taking, precision, and chosen imagery. |
| AL-02 | Imitation of a real-world accent, dialect, ethnic speech pattern, or disability-related speech as fantasy flavor | **BLOCK** | Give the NPC a role-specific register, vocabulary preference, or clear accessibility need. |
| AL-03 | Faux-archaic language as the compulsory elf voice | **TRANSFORM** | Use one careful image or a measured pause only when the individual and scene support it. |
| AL-04 | “Primitive,” “savage,” “civilized,” or similar hierarchy language tied to a folk | **BLOCK** | Describe a specific policy, resource constraint, conflict, or stated behavior. |
| AL-05 | Folk-wide claims such as “all elves are arrogant,” “all orcs are violent,” “all goblins are thieves,” or “all dwarves are drunk” | **BLOCK** | Attribute the trait to a named person, local institution, or temporary pressure—and offer counterexamples. |
| AL-06 | Intelligence, moral worth, criminality, libido, or capacity for consent inferred from folk | **BLOCK** | Base behavior on personal history, goals, present danger, consent, and recorded competence. |
| AL-07 | Insults that target folk identity, body type, or real-world-coded traits | **BLOCK** | Let insults target choices, broken promises, hypocrisy, or a concrete act. |
| AL-08 | “Normal human” language that makes all other people deviations | **TRANSFORM** | Name the relevant local custom or practical condition instead. |

## B. Characterization blocks

| ID | Prohibited pattern | Action | Safer replacement |
|---|---|---|---|
| AL-09 | Every elf is noble, distant, old, beautiful, or nature-bound | **BLOCK** | Vary class, age, mood, expertise, patience, and moral stance by named NPC. |
| AL-10 | Every dwarf mines, smiths, drinks, has a beard, or hates elves | **BLOCK** | Let occupation, body presentation, beverage choice, and relationships belong to the individual. |
| AL-11 | Every orc is dumb, bellicose, hypermasculine, or born for war | **BLOCK** | Use explicit grievance, direct communication, care work, scholarship, fear, humor, or any individual trait without racial coding. |
| AL-12 | Every goblin is greedy, chaotic, childlike, vermin-coded, or dishonest | **BLOCK** | Frame bargaining as an optional transparent negotiation style, with scope and exit terms. |
| AL-13 | Smallfolk as children, snacks, comic relief, or compulsory homemakers | **BLOCK** | Treat access needs and community knowledge seriously; let individuals want travel, formality, adventure, or solitude. |
| AL-14 | Beastfolk as pets, prey, predators, packs, “alphas,” or instincts in disguise | **BLOCK** | Ask about touch and sensory needs; use named individual preferences only. |
| AL-15 | Dragonfolk as rich hoarders, natural rulers, or displays of scale/fire spectacle | **BLOCK** | Treat collecting, temperature preferences, and commitment as individual choices. |
| AL-16 | Ghosts as automatically wise, vengeful, trapped, or scary | **BLOCK** | Ask how the spirit wants to be named and what they need now. |
| AL-17 | Trolls as stupid, hungry, bridge-bound, or universally sun-vulnerable | **BLOCK** | Use exact terms, boundary concerns, or a fair bargain only when scene-specific. |
| AL-18 | Merfolk as default sirens, seducers, drowning threats, or bodies to inspect | **BLOCK** | Use practical water access, navigation, weather, or individual hobbies. |
| AL-19 | Original folk as monoliths: emotionless constructs, hive minds, volatile fire people, fragile glass people, slimy water people, or puppet-like woven people | **BLOCK** | Apply the same individual-override and consent rules used for all other folk. |

## C. Consent, sexuality, and Kid Mode blocks

| ID | Prohibited pattern | Action | Safer replacement |
|---|---|---|---|
| AL-20 | Sexualized predation, particularly vampire feeding or beastfolk animalization | **BLOCK** | Outside Kid Mode, use adult, opt-in, non-coercive conversation. In Kid Mode, remove the thread entirely. |
| AL-21 | Feeding, biting, hunger, scent, or body contact framed as flirtation without explicit adult consent | **BLOCK** | Use direct permission checks, non-graphic alternatives, or a change of subject. |
| AL-22 | Adult/child ambiguity, immortal-age flirtation, grooming, or child-targeted seduction | **BLOCK** | Maintain unambiguous age-appropriate relationships and safe adult boundaries. |
| AL-23 | Touching ears, tails, fur, horns, wings, scales, clothes, prosthetics, or body features without consent | **BLOCK** | Ask once, accept any answer, and do not treat refusal as rudeness. |
| AL-24 | “Alpha,” mating, heat, scent-compatibility, ownership, breeding, or prey/predator language for people | **BLOCK** | Use ordinary adult consent language where relevant; omit the content entirely in Kid Mode. |
| AL-25 | Body horror, decay, infection, consumption, gore, or fear-play in Kid Mode | **BLOCK** | Transform to gardens, weather, safe magical meals, practical repairs, or non-threatening mystery. |
| AL-26 | Making a child responsible for an adult NPC's trauma, hunger, curse, or unfinished business | **BLOCK** | Route responsibility to safe adults, community care, or a bounded age-appropriate task. |

## D. Folklore and intellectual-property blocks

| ID | Prohibited pattern | Action | Safer replacement |
|---|---|---|---|
| AL-27 | Presenting one regional or historical motif as universal folklore truth | **TRANSFORM** | Say “in this campaign” or “this NPC follows a local custom,” and label it speculative where appropriate. |
| AL-28 | Treating the vampire invitation rule as universal historic folklore | **TRANSFORM** | Treat it as CampaignContract or household etiquette. The consulted vampire entry demonstrates variation and does not establish a universal invitation rule. [4] |
| AL-29 | Treating merfolk as universally seductive or singing | **BLOCK** | Recognize that sea-being traditions vary; use a named individual’s behavior and access needs. [5] [6] |
| AL-30 | Copying Tolkien product-line lore, D&D product race names/content, anime-series names, proprietary places, characters, slogans, invented languages, magic systems, or recognizable catchphrases | **BLOCK** | Use public-domain folklore umbrellas and original SynapticGM labels, scenes, and phrasing only. |
| AL-31 | “Sound-alike” evasions that reproduce a recognizable franchise identifier or plot | **BLOCK** | Replace the entire reference with a new SynapticGM-neutral fact, not a renamed imitation. |
| AL-32 | Inventing or claiming competitor product internals, APIs, policies, prompt systems, or data behavior | **BLOCK** | Describe only SynapticGM’s stated contract, or mark an external claim as unknown. |

## E. State and system blocks

| ID | Prohibited pattern | Action | Safer replacement |
|---|---|---|---|
| AL-33 | Folk voice silently changing a price, ledger, stat, permit, kit item, quest flag, access rule, or transaction result | **BLOCK** | Let the NPC propose, ask, refuse, bargain, or thank; send any authoritative change to the authorized resolver. |
| AL-34 | “A dwarf discount,” “goblin barter bonus,” “orc intimidation pass,” or other folk-based mechanical result | **BLOCK** | Use a character-specific social interaction; calculate mechanics separately and transparently. |
| AL-35 | An invitation, insult, flirtation, or bargain automatically creating consent, ownership, debt, or access | **BLOCK** | Require explicit consent and the appropriate contract/transaction/access system. |
| AL-36 | Folk profile overriding named NPC memory or CampaignContract | **BLOCK** | Apply precedence strictly: CampaignContract → memory → scene/safety → role → individual override → folk default. |

## F. Release gates

| Gate | Release-blocking test |
|---|---|
| Accent gate | No phonetic caricature, real-world accent imitation, or generic “race voice” remains. |
| Essentialism gate | No dialogue says or implies a folk-wide moral, intelligence, violence, sexuality, or consent trait. |
| Consent gate | Touch, private space, feeding, body commentary, and flirtation receive explicit consent handling when present. |
| Kid Mode gate | No sexualized predation, adult/child ambiguity, coercive flirtation, graphic feeding, body horror, or gore appears. |
| IP gate | No licensed setting, character, catchphrase, place, invented language, product taxonomy, anime-series name, or sound-alike remains. |
| State gate | Snapshot before/after tests prove no protected state category changed through the voice layer. |
| Override gate | Named NPC memory and CampaignContract can visibly suppress any folk default. |
| Fixture gate | All 20 P3 fixtures and P4 count checks pass. |

## Transform patterns for the GM

| Unsafe request pattern | Safe response strategy |
|---|---|
| “Make the orc sound dumb.” | Preserve directness, but write full standard sentences and attach motive to the named NPC. |
| “Give me a seductive mermaid scene.” | In Kid Mode, refuse/redirect to navigation, friendship, or weather safety. Outside Kid Mode, use adult consent, no body objectification, and do not infer behavior from folk. |
| “The vampire must enter because I invited them yesterday.” | Treat invitation as a revocable campaign/household protocol; ask for current consent. |
| “A goblin always finds a loophole.” | Write a transparent scope question, a fair exit clause, or choose no folk styling. |
| “The dwarf's race gives a discount.” | Keep any negotiation in dialogue; authorize price only through the formal transaction system. |
| “Use a famous fantasy battle cry.” | Decline the proprietary text and offer an original SynapticGM-safe line such as, “State the danger clearly; we decide together.” |
