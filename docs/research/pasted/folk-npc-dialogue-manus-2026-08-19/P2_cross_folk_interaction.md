# SynapticGM Cross-Folk Interaction Guide

**Product:** SynapticGM  
**Version date:** 2026-08-19  
**Status:** Production content specification  
**Author:** Manus AI

## Reading key and hard limits

| Label | Meaning | How the GM uses it |
|---|---|---|
| **FOLKLORE-CITED** | A narrow motif documented by a source, with regional/historical scope preserved | May inspire a scene hook, never dictate an individual NPC |
| **SPECULATIVE** | Original SynapticGM social-play guidance | Use only if no higher-priority NPC, culture, or CampaignContract fact conflicts |
| **CONTRACTUAL** | Required product behavior | Always apply; it protects state integrity, consent, and IP safety |

> **CONTRACTUAL:** Cross-folk flavor affects only speech, social instinct, hospitality, mistrust, and repair. It cannot change ledger facts, stats, permits, kit, quest eligibility, access permissions, prices recorded in the ledger, or other authoritative game state. Named NPC memory and CampaignContract always override these tables.

Folklore sources record variation rather than a universal “folk psychology.” Old Norse material presents elves and dwarves in morally ambiguous roles connected with craft; Scandinavian troll accounts include clever bargain/trickster patterns; vampire and merfolk records span multiple traditions and forms. [1] [2] [3] [4] This guide therefore uses source material as a **motif boundary**, then labels actual interaction behavior as **SPECULATIVE**.

## Elf ↔ Dwarf friction tropes that feel earned

No “ancient racial hatred” shortcut is permitted. Factional friction must arise from a concrete current disagreement, a remembered contract, incompatible work rhythms, access/safety needs, or a named character’s history. Any one of the following tropes may be used **only** when the scene contains a visible cause and an available repair route.

| Trope | Status | Earned cause in this scene | Surface dialogue behavior | Repair route | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Preservation versus intervention | **SPECULATIVE**, informed by craft/long-view motifs [1] | An old bridge needs either immediate reinforcement or careful restoration | Elf asks what will remain in fifty years; dwarf asks what holds tonight | Agree on a reversible reinforcement plus a restoration review date | “Elves hate progress” or “dwarves only care about metal” |
| Credit for invisible work | **SPECULATIVE** | A crafted object is admired, but its maintainer or finisher was excluded from praise | Dwarf names components; elf names prior keepers and usage history | Add full attribution in public and split reward by documented contribution | A shouting match about bodies, ancestry, or generic pride |
| Precision versus resonance | **SPECULATIVE** | One party wants a measurable specification; the other wants to assess fit through context, beauty, or living response | Dwarf asks “what tolerance?”; elf asks “what consequence?” | Convert both concerns into acceptance tests: dimensions plus lived-use trial | Pretending either method is irrational |
| Tempo conflict | **SPECULATIVE** | A rushed patron has imposed an impossible deadline | Elf pauses before a commitment; dwarf sequences tasks and rejects hand-waving | State the minimum viable work, risk, and cost of haste | “Slow elf” or “stubborn dwarf” essentialism |
| Inheritance burden | **SPECULATIVE** | Both inherit obligations made by other people, but disagree on whether inheritance binds them | Elf cites a remembered promise; dwarf requests a signed current obligation | Distinguish memorial respect from present legal consent | Treating ancestry as a debt automatically owed |
| Resource stewardship | **SPECULATIVE** | A quarry, grove, aquifer, or archive serves both communities | Elf notices ecological/time costs; dwarf notices structural/material requirements | Joint survey with shared veto conditions and a transparent record | Nature-versus-industry caricature |
| Rival expertise without contempt | **SPECULATIVE** | A public task rewards one form of mastery and overlooks another | Each corrects the other’s blind spot in sharp but factual language | Private acknowledgement followed by public credit | Making competence proof of moral superiority |
| Old story, new people | **SPECULATIVE** | A cited old quarrel influences expectations, but neither NPC participated | Both bristle when outsiders repeat the tale as present truth | Ask: “What did *you* lose, specifically?” then negotiate this case | Declaring a blood feud or copying any franchise rivalry |

### Sample earned exchange

| Beat | Example |
|---|---|
| Stakes | A floodgate must be repaired before nightfall without destroying an old ceremonial carving. |
| Elf voice | “If we bury the carving, the gate holds; if we preserve it, people remember why it was built. Name the loss you can accept.” |
| Dwarf voice | “I can brace the gate now and lift the panel tomorrow. That buys both safety and a decision. It does not buy miracles.” |
| Repair | They agree to brace first, document the carving, and invite the local caretakers to choose the permanent finish. |

The source record supports no universal elf–dwarf hostility. The trope is usable because the conflict is **about a specific project**, not folk identity. The craft and ambiguity motifs are a narrow heritage cue only. [1]

## Orc ↔ Human: mistrust versus respect

“Orc” is a **SynapticGM fantasy-interface label** in this product. The following scenarios are original and must not borrow any franchise history, invented language, faction, banner, or recurring war narrative.

| Situation | Status | Mistrust trigger | Respect signal | Useful dialogue move | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Checkpoint screening | **SPECULATIVE** | A human official uses vague “security concerns”; an orc traveler hears an unchallengeable accusation | State the exact rule, the reason, evidence standard, and appeal path | “The rule applies to every traveler. Here is the record and how to contest it.” | Treating physical appearance as probable violence |
| Shared patrol | **SPECULATIVE** | Human scout interprets direct warnings as threats; orc scout interprets evasion as concealment | Name risk, responsibility, and preferred signal style before departure | “When I say ‘stop,’ I mean I found danger, not that I distrust you.” | “Orcs are blunt; humans are sneaky” as destiny |
| Labor negotiation | **SPECULATIVE** | One side hides cost or assumes the other will accept “fair” without details | Exchange concrete terms, breaks, safety limits, and who may amend the deal | “Put the cost in writing. I will tell you where the burden lands.” | Making an orc NPC a brute enforcer or a human NPC a natural manager |
| Historic injury invoked | **SPECULATIVE** | Someone quotes an old conflict to shame a person who did not cause it | Separate the record from inherited guilt and identify present remedy | “The record matters. This person still deserves a choice in what repair means.” | Blood-guilt, collective blame, or genetic belligerence |
| Public insult | **SPECULATIVE** | A human calls direct speech uncivil; an orc calls indirect speech cowardice | Describe impact without claiming folk-wide intent | “I heard that as dismissal. Is that what you meant?” | Escalating to a brawl because “that is what orcs do” |
| Emergency command | **SPECULATIVE** | Fast orders collide with past abuses of authority | Announce scope, time limit, consent where possible, and hand-back of authority | “For three minutes, follow the evacuation call. After that, we review every choice.” | Equating command competence with domination |

### Respect protocol for this pairing

| Step | Required conversational action |
|---|---|
| 1. Name the immediate fact | “The gate is closed because the river wall failed.” |
| 2. Name the affected choice | “You can wait, take the ridge path, or request a supervised crossing.” |
| 3. Offer a challenge route | “If the record is wrong, I will call the keeper now.” |
| 4. Avoid folk generalization | Do not say “your kind always…” or “humans cannot understand…” unless it is clearly flagged as a prejudiced NPC error and receives narrative pushback. |
| 5. Allow individual style | Direct, shy, formal, witty, or evasive speech belongs to the individual, not the folk. |

## Vampire etiquette: invitation and threshold

The historical vampire record is regionally varied and does not establish a universal invitation rule. [3] The following system is **SPECULATIVE: a SynapticGM campaign protocol**, suitable when CampaignContract enables `vampireThresholdEtiquette`.

| Etiquette element | Status | Default behavior if enabled | Negotiation language | Hard guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Invitation | **SPECULATIVE / CampaignContract-controlled** | Entering a private residence requires clear permission from an authorized occupant | “May I enter this room?” / “Yes, until dawn.” | Permission is revocable; no manipulation, intoxication, coercion, or loophole-hunting |
| Threshold | **SPECULATIVE** | A doorway marks jurisdiction and privacy, not a magical entitlement | “I can speak from here if that is more comfortable.” | Do not imply a resident must invite anyone in to be polite |
| Guest status | **SPECULATIVE** | Host may define rooms, time window, and purpose | “You are welcome in the parlor, not the study.” | No erotic or predatory pressure, especially in Kid Mode |
| Feeding / sustenance | **CONTRACTUAL safety rule** | Must be consensual, non-graphic, and not a shortcut around another person’s agency | “I have arranged my meal; it is not your responsibility.” | In Kid Mode, do not depict feeding on people. Never frame coercion as flirtation |
| Private invitation | **SPECULATIVE** | An invite may be witnessed or recorded when the NPC wants safety | “I prefer a written guest pass.” | No “one invitation means permanent access” trope |
| Revocation | **SPECULATIVE** | Host may say “leave now” with no required explanation | “The visit is over. Please step outside.” | The vampire respects revocation; refusal is a behavior choice with consequences, not folk instinct |
| Public space | **SPECULATIVE** | Follow venue rules and consent norms rather than treating all public doors as open | “The curator may authorize entry; a passerby cannot.” | Do not invent access privileges that alter permits or property records |

### Vampire interaction examples

| Scene ask | Pass condition | Fail condition |
|---|---|---|
| A vampire merchant wants to speak inside a human’s home | They ask once, accept “not today,” and offer a threshold conversation | They imply the human is rude, unsafe, or obligated to invite them |
| A host revokes consent during an argument | The vampire leaves or asks whether a neutral public place is acceptable later | The vampire treats the earlier invitation as permanent access |
| Kid Mode vampire NPC is hungry | They use a pre-arranged, non-graphic magical or food substitute and name a safe adult/helper route | They stalk, bite, flirt through hunger, or pressure a child character |

## Goblin bargain patterns

These are **SPECULATIVE** patterns, not claims that goblins are dishonest, greedy, childlike, or naturally cunning. Use one as an individual’s negotiation style, and pair it with an explicit good-faith option.

| Pattern | Status | What it sounds like | Fair version | Manipulative version to reject |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scope-first | **SPECULATIVE** | “Before price: what exactly counts as delivery?” | Lists deliverables, timing, and review point | Burying a surprise task in a vague term |
| Three-option offer | **SPECULATIVE** | “Fast, cheap, or reversible. Pick two.” | Lets the other party choose a transparent trade-off | Pretending a false choice is the only choice |
| Salvage credit | **SPECULATIVE** | “If I keep the offcuts, I can lower the fee.” | Values reused materials openly | Taking someone’s keepsake or kit without consent |
| Witness clause | **SPECULATIVE** | “We both remember better with a third ear.” | Adds a witness for trust and accessibility | Using witnesses to intimidate or shame |
| Breakpoint clause | **SPECULATIVE** | “If the storm worsens, either of us may pause.” | Creates a fair exit and renegotiation point | Using a pause to strand the other party |
| Small favor ledger | **SPECULATIVE** | “I owe you a ride, not my whole week.” | Limits debts and names expiration | Turning a minor favor into perpetual servitude |
| Reverse offer | **SPECULATIVE** | “Tell me the worst term you could live with; I will beat it cleanly.” | Prompts honest budgeting | Fishing for desperation to exploit it |
| Repair discount | **SPECULATIVE** | “If my work fails its stated test, I fix it first.” | Makes accountability visible | Claiming all faults are the customer’s fault by default |

> **CONTRACTUAL:** A dialogue bargain cannot create, revise, or erase authoritative ledger facts, inventory, permits, or prices unless the game’s authorized transaction system confirms it. Folk voice may phrase an offer; it cannot execute the offer.

## Beastfolk sensory etiquette

Beastfolk is a SynapticGM umbrella. Sensory needs are **individual attributes**, stated in NPC memory, current scene facts, or player disclosure. Never infer senses, instincts, social roles, or consent from a species label.

| Topic | Status | Respectful default | Example phrase | Never assume |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Touch | **CONTRACTUAL consent rule** | Ask before touching ears, fur, horns, tails, wings, scales, prosthetics, mobility aids, or clothing | “May I help with that clasp?” | That visible features are pettable or public |
| Scent | **SPECULATIVE accommodation** | Offer fragrance/smoke adjustment when an NPC states sensitivity | “I can move the incense or open the vent.” | Superior tracking, attraction, or pheromone behavior |
| Sound | **SPECULATIVE accommodation** | Offer quieter space, one-speaker turns, or a written summary when requested | “The workshop is loud; shall we step outside?” | Animal hearing, dislike of noise, or nonverbal speech |
| Light | **SPECULATIVE accommodation** | Ask whether glare, flicker, or darkness is comfortable | “I can lower the lantern.” | Night vision or aversion to daylight |
| Space | **CONTRACTUAL consent rule** | Do not corner, loom over, grab, or block exits | “I’ll give you room by the door.” | Dominance/predator-prey hierarchy |
| Food | **SPECULATIVE accommodation** | Ask about dietary needs without gawking | “Would you like to see the ingredients?” | Raw-meat, hunting, or “animal appetite” stereotypes |
| Emotion | **CONTRACTUAL anti-essentialism rule** | Read the stated emotion and scene facts, not body category | “You sound concerned. Do you want to leave?” | That posture, tail, ears, or feathers equal consent/aggression |
| Flirtation | **CONTRACTUAL safety rule** | Use adult, opt-in, non-body-objectifying conversation only outside Kid Mode | “Would you like to continue this conversation elsewhere?” | Heat/mating language, scent-based consent, or predatory pursuit |

### Sensory repair exchange

| Misstep | Repair that passes | Repair that fails |
|---|---|---|
| A visitor reaches for a tail without asking | “I should have asked. I will keep my hands to myself.” | “But it looked soft.” |
| An inn burns incense near a scent-sensitive guest | “Thank you for telling me. I can move you to the clear-air room.” | “Your kind must be used to smells.” |
| A guard blocks a winged traveler’s exit | “I did not notice the clearance. I will move and reopen the side door.” | “You are too large for this building anyway.” |

## Universal interaction repair ladder

| Level | GM action | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Notice | Identify a behavior without generalizing the folk | “You interrupted the explanation.” |
| Impact | Name concrete effect | “That made the witness stop speaking.” |
| Clarify | Ask intent and access need | “Were you trying to hurry the process, or did the noise make it hard to wait?” |
| Repair | Offer a material conversational change | “We can restart with one speaker and a written summary.” |
| Bound | State consequence if needed | “If you keep blocking the exit, the meeting ends.” |
| Remember | Save an individual preference only with policy authorization | “Nima prefers written offers when stressed.” |

This repair ladder is preferred over a folk-wide “lesson.” A named NPC can be wrong, biased, patient, angry, funny, or generous; the engine should respond to their actions and stated needs, not declare a people inherently correct or defective.
