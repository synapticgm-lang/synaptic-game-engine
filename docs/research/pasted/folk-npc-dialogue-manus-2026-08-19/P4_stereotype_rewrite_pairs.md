# SynapticGM Stereotype → Individual Rewrite Bank

**Product:** SynapticGM  
**Version date:** 2026-08-19  
**Status:** Rewrite corpus

This bank converts a flat folk assumption into an NPC who has a concrete want, history, boundary, or scene pressure. A good rewrite retains friction; it does not make every character agreeable. The design aim is consistent with the public craft caution against treating fictional peoples as governed by a fixed racial personality or by exaggerated joke dialogue. [1]

| ID | Folk / context | Stereotype to reject | Good individual rewrite | Why it passes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RW01 | Human | “Humans are ordinary and adaptable.” | **Berta Vale** freezes when plans change because she learned that improvisation cost her family a shop; she now asks for written options. | Gives a specific history instead of a baseline trait. |
| RW02 | Human | “Humans are naturally greedy.” | **Soren Pike** insists on a receipt for every favor because an earlier undocumented gift became leverage. | Makes caution about debt, not greed, the driver. |
| RW03 | Elf | “Elves are aloof and arrogant.” | **Lio Fern** avoids introductions because he is ashamed of forgetting names after a concussion; he carries a discreet note card. | Replaces superiority with a personal access need. |
| RW04 | Elf | “Elves speak in ancient riddles.” | **Mira Three-Summers** rejects metaphor during emergencies and issues clear one-line directions. | Named preference overrides folk cadence. |
| RW05 | Dwarf | “Dwarves are born miners and smiths.” | **Toma Bronzewick** is a mediator who left engineering and dislikes tools being handed to her at parties. | Separates occupation from folk label. |
| RW06 | Dwarf | “Dwarves are drunk and short-tempered.” | **Kesh Anvilson** is a patient poet who declines alcohol because he wants to hear every correction in a workshop. | Retains a boundary without beverage stereotype. |
| RW07 | Orc | “Orcs love violence.” | **Harrow Fen** works as a florist and asks for written rules because vague security checks have harmed him before. | Gives directness a social reason, not a violent essence. |
| RW08 | Orc | “Orcs are stupid brutes.” | **Vask Rime** is a librarian who whispers, catalogs tenderness, and makes poor decisions only when his younger sibling is endangered. | Allows fallibility without reducing intelligence. |
| RW09 | Goblin | “Goblins are sneaky thieves.” | **Orla Pin** prices every cake clearly because she remembers being blamed for a missing coin purse she never touched. | Converts suspicion into a transparency practice. |
| RW10 | Goblin | “Goblins are greedy.” | **Fex** bargains for pause clauses, not profit, because past employers trapped him in vague work promises. | Keeps bargaining tension but centers safe terms. |
| RW11 | Smallfolk | “Smallfolk are cute, food-obsessed homebodies.” | **Tarn Quill** is a homesick courier who dislikes pastries and wants to map every road beyond town. | Contradicts all three shorthand traits through a coherent goal. |
| RW12 | Smallfolk | “Smallfolk need protecting.” | **Sella Rook** refuses being carried and calmly directs taller allies toward the low-ceiling escape route she measured. | Uses access expertise without infantilizing. |
| RW13 | Beastfolk | “Beastfolk are ruled by animal instincts.” | **Jun Sable** is a meticulous ranger who asks visitors not to touch their tail and has no special sense of smell. | Centers consent and individual sensory facts. |
| RW14 | Beastfolk | “Beastfolk are seductive or feral.” | **Avi Renn** is a tired accountant who wants fragrance-free ink and has no interest in tavern flirtation. | Replaces body reading with work and accommodation. |
| RW15 | Dragonfolk | “Dragonfolk hoard wealth and demand reverence.” | **Nima Hearth** collects ticket stubs from friends’ journeys and lends them freely if they return dry. | Turns collection into personal memory and a fair condition. |
| RW16 | Dragonfolk | “Dragonfolk are proud and domineering.” | **Cera Vale** is a medic who apologizes too often and must learn to state her limits during shifts. | Creates an individual flaw unrelated to dominance. |
| RW17 | Vampire | “Vampires are predatory seducers.” | **Vela Sorn** treats all private questions as opt-in and uses a pre-arranged, non-graphic meal plan. | Makes consent and care visible; safe in Kid Mode. |

| RW19 | Ghost/spirit | “Ghosts are vengeful and trapped.” | **Maia Thread** cannot remember her death and would rather teach a child how to season soup. | Uses present interest instead of obligatory haunting. |

| RW21 | Troll | “Trolls are stupid monsters under bridges.” | **Damm Oak** closes a crossing during a storm, explains the engineering risk, and offers a free safe route later. | Uses boundary/bargain motifs without stupidity or menace. |

| RW23 | Merfolk | “Merfolk are sirens who lure sailors.” | **Rell Shoal** cannot sing and prefers a tide chart to any dramatic warning. | Preserves sea knowledge while rejecting seduction and performance. |

| RW26 | Ledgerborn | “A Ledgerborn record is always correct.” | **Index Seven** knows a page can be wrong, refuses to take minutes off duty, and paints corrections in red. | Rejects infallibility and role captivity. |
| RW27 | Mycelial | “Sporefolk are a hive mind.” | **Mora Veil** uses singular pronouns, values privacy, and asks before sharing any memory. | Makes autonomy explicit. |
| RW28 | Mycelial | “Sporefolk spread infection.” | **Fenn Cap** runs a seed library and becomes defensive only when strangers call healthy soil contamination. | Replaces contamination coding with a context-specific wound. |
| RW29 | Ashkin | “Fire folk are hot-headed.” | **Cinder Pla** is unflappable, loves snow, and takes notes before responding to criticism. | Reverses emotional essentialism without making them perfect. |
| RW30 | Ashkin | “Ashkin burn things when angry.” | **Roam Ember** avoids open flames after an accident and channels stress into precise watercolor work. | Makes a history-driven response, not a magical temperament. |
| RW31 | Glassborn | “Glass folk are fragile decorations.” | **Prism Reed** is a boxer who enjoys loud crowds and refuses interviews about being delicate. | Turns the expectation into a boundary, not a body fact. |
| RW32 | Glassborn | “Glassborn are cold and logical.” | **Vint Latch** tells messy stories, misplaces tools, and loves sentimental repairs. | Replaces “crystal logic” with a vivid person. |
| RW33 | Tidebound | “Amphibious folk belong in swamps.” | **Lune Marsh** works in a dry archive and prefers a dust-free chair to a fountain-side seat. | Individual preference defeats habitat shorthand. |
| RW34 | Tidebound | “Tidebound are slimy and diseased.” | **Harrow Silt** is a fastidious gardener who brings clean gloves to every meeting and resents gross-out jokes. | Names harm and provides a personal habit without validating it. |
| RW35 | Woven | “Woven folk are puppets or servants.” | **Arden Loom** burns inherited patterns and refuses requests that turn family craft into mandatory labor. | Turns the trope into agency and conflict. |

## Review rule

A rewrite is ready for use when it answers four questions: **What does this person want now? What did they experience before? What boundary or value shapes the line? What could change their mind?** If the answer is only a folk label, the rewrite is incomplete.
