# SynapticGM Folk Voice Expectation Matrix

**Product:** SynapticGM  
**Version date:** 2026-08-19  
**Status:** Production content specification  
**Author:** Manus AI

## Use, evidence, and precedence

This is a **voice-and-social-instinct layer only**. It may guide diction, turn-taking, metaphors, boundaries, and first-impression behavior. It must **never** modify ledger facts, stats, permits, inventory, equipment, access rules, or other game-state truth. A familiar folk label signals a player-facing expectation, not an essential nature.

> **Precedence:** CampaignContract → named NPC memory and explicit biography → current scene facts and consent/safety requirements → local culture/role → individual override → optional folk default → no special voice.

Every profile below is **SPECULATIVE: SynapticGM original play guidance**, unless a sentence bears a narrow **FOLKLORE-CITED** note. Folklore sources show regional variation, changing histories, and ambiguity—not a single correct personality. For example, one Norse reference describes elves and dwarves as skilled and morally ambiguous, while an encyclopedia entry records troll trickster-and-bargain traditions. These motifs are prompts, not species laws. [1] [2] The anti-caricature model follows the design principle that culture is not the same as race and that exaggerated joke accents should be avoided. [3]

## 1. Human

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Speech cadence | **SPECULATIVE.** Match region, profession, age, and present stakes rather than “humanity.” Default to plain, adaptable turns; allow interruptions only when the individual’s role or emotion supports them. |
| Metaphor palette | Local work, weather, family sayings, civic life, tools, food, or faith—selected from the NPC’s biography. Avoid treating one real-world culture as the human default. |
| Social defaults (respect, insult, hospitality, fear) | Respect is often legible through names, attention, and reciprocity; insults target choices rather than ancestry; hospitality is negotiated by host custom; fear may trigger questions, deference, flight, anger, or humor. None is universal. |
| How they talk to humans / to each other | To humans, use shared local context without assuming agreement. To other humans, reveal subculture through what they take for granted, not through generic “ordinary person” speech. |
| Kid Mode differences | Use clear boundaries, repair after rudeness, and low-intensity disagreement. Remove sexual bargaining, cruelty, and threats of social exclusion. |
| Never-lines | “Humans are the normal race”; real-world imitation accents; treating nationality, class, or disability as a joke; universal claims about human greed or cowardice. |
| Individual override examples (3) | **Aster Venn**, archivist, speaks in clipped catalog labels because she hates wasted time. **Pell**, ferry cook, offers food before questions because of a personal rescue debt. **Marshal Iri**, human, refuses eye contact after a recent injury; it is trauma context, not folk behavior. |

## 2. Elf

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Speech cadence | **SPECULATIVE.** Measured, image-rich, and comfortable with a short pause before commitment; reduce formality under pressure. Do not use faux-archaic grammar or a copied accent. |
| Metaphor palette | Light through leaves, seasons, craftwork, long maintenance, echoes, and names. **FOLKLORE-CITED boundary:** Norse material includes skill, precious objects, and ambiguity, not automatic grace or virtue. [1] |
| Social defaults (respect, insult, hospitality, fear) | Respect may mean remembering a preference or finishing a thought accurately; insult may be precise understatement rather than a slur; hospitality may emphasize shared attention; fear may appear as intensified caution, not superiority. |
| How they talk to humans / to each other | To humans, explain a long-view concern without patronizing: “The bridge can wait one hour; the flood cannot.” To each other, allow shorthand built from shared history, but make it comprehensible in play. |
| Kid Mode differences | Make patience and curiosity visible; turn grief or distance into a request for quiet, not aloof contempt. No romance-coded age gaps or “ancient seducer” framing. |
| Never-lines | “I am too old to explain”; all-elves-arrogant claims; pointy-ear jokes as a personality substitute; invented franchise language, titles, or place names. |
| Individual override examples (3) | **Sael Porrin**, elf courier, talks fast because deadlines matter more than ceremony. **Mira-of-Three-Summers**, elf gardener, distrusts metaphor and asks concrete questions. **Orren Vale**, elf veteran, openly laughs first in tense negotiations to prevent people treating him as untouchable. |

## 3. Dwarf

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Speech cadence | **SPECULATIVE.** Deliberate and concrete, with a preference for named materials, clear sequence, and promises that can be checked. Do not write a comic “gruff” accent. |
| Metaphor palette | Joinery, load-bearing work, heat, tools, tested seams, and repaired objects. **FOLKLORE-CITED boundary:** Old Norse sources support crafted precious objects and moral ambiguity; they do not make every dwarf a miner, smith, or beer drinker. [1] |
| Social defaults (respect, insult, hospitality, fear) | Respect means crediting work and honoring a stated limit; insult is claiming effort did not matter; hospitality may be an offer of repair, a warm seat, or practical instruction; fear can sharpen audit questions. |
| How they talk to humans / to each other | To humans, translate expertise without condescension: “The hinge is sound; the frame is not.” To each other, compressed task language may emerge during work, but social talk can be lyrical, shy, or chaotic by individual. |
| Kid Mode differences | Make competence cooperative: ask a child to help check a safe detail or choose a repair patch. Exclude alcohol jokes, physical intimidation, and “short-tempered” inevitability. |
| Never-lines | Beard/body jokes; “born for mining”; automatic hostility to elves; drunkenness as identity; pseudo-Scottish or any real-world accent spelling. |
| Individual override examples (3) | **Toma Bronzewick**, dwarf mediator, speaks softly and avoids technical language after leaving a guild. **Kesh Anvilson**, dwarf poet, treats every invoice as verse. **Rul Vey**, dwarf engineer, hates being asked to repair things because she is on holiday. |

## 4. Orc

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Speech cadence | **SPECULATIVE; original SynapticGM profile.** Direct clauses, named stakes, and an invitation to state objections plainly. Directness is not aggression and may coexist with gentleness or verbosity. |
| Metaphor palette | Weather fronts, trail markers, shelter, knots, scars as history, drills, and chosen kin. These are setting-neutral prompts, not inherited instincts. |
| Social defaults (respect, insult, hospitality, fear) | Respect may mean saying the difficult thing without evasion; insult may be refusing to name a grievance; hospitality may include a clearly marked shared table; fear may prompt perimeter-checking or a request for terms. |
| How they talk to humans / to each other | To humans, avoid “human” as a taunt. Offer clarity: “Say whether you need help, witness, or distance.” To each other, disagreement can be frank but should include an exit ramp or repair ritual chosen by the NPC. |
| Kid Mode differences | Recast combat bravado as safe bravery: asking for help, standing beside someone, or naming fear. No “savage,” bloodthirst, or intimidation-by-body-size framing. |
| Never-lines | Broken grammar; “all orcs love war”; racialized physical caricature; intelligence jokes; claims of innate criminality; franchise battle cries or clan lore. |
| Individual override examples (3) | **Vask Rime**, orc librarian, whispers and apologizes for taking up shelf space. **Harrow Fen**, orc florist, communicates in extravagant compliments. **Captain Om**, orc guard, avoids direct conflict because he is rebuilding trust after a command failure. |

## 5. Goblin

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Speech cadence | **SPECULATIVE.** Quick option-scanning, playful reframing, and crisp questions about scope. Use standard grammar; speed is a choice, not a “chitter.” |
| Metaphor palette | Pockets, hinges, leftovers, shortcuts, overlooked paths, scrap value, and tiny margins. Use sparingly, and never equate scarcity with dishonesty. |
| Social defaults (respect, insult, hospitality, fear) | Respect may mean asking what is negotiable before assuming; insult may be an unpriced demand; hospitality can be a neatly labeled shared cache; fear can become over-preparation, jokes, or a request for a written deal. |
| How they talk to humans / to each other | To humans, explain an offer’s hidden costs openly. To each other, they may compare contingencies or enjoy wordplay, but do not make secrecy obligatory. |
| Kid Mode differences | Make bargaining turn-taking practice: “You choose first; I choose second.” Avoid theft jokes, hoarding pathology, and menace. |
| Never-lines | “Sneaky by nature”; vermin imagery; clipped comic accent; child-coded adult treatment; universal greed; any equation of small stature with criminality. |
| Individual override examples (3) | **Nib Sato**, goblin magistrate, refuses bargains and insists on public process. **Fex**, goblin scout, is brutally literal and cannot stand jokes in a crisis. **Orla Pin**, goblin baker, gives away surplus because she remembers hunger. |

## 6. Smallfolk

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Speech cadence | **SPECULATIVE; generic SynapticGM label.** Warm but not twee; practical observations followed by an invitation. A smallfolk NPC may be terse, grandiose, anxious, or formal like anyone else. |
| Metaphor palette | Hearths, routes, gardens, weatherproofing, shared meals, small repairs, and neighborhood rhythms. Never use food as a compulsory personality trait. |
| Social defaults (respect, insult, hospitality, fear) | Respect can mean not talking over them or reaching for their belongings without asking; insult can be treating care work as trivial; hospitality is consent-based and may be an offered seat, map, or snack; fear may favor evacuation planning. |
| How they talk to humans / to each other | To taller folk, name access needs rather than pleading: “Lower the lantern; I cannot see past its glare.” To each other, local shorthand can show community without implying insularity. |
| Kid Mode differences | Center safe belonging and resource-sharing. Do not use adult/child confusion, patronizing language, or infantilized romance. |
| Never-lines | Food-thief jokes; “cute but useless”; baby-talk; all-smallfolk-homebodies; proprietary terminology, geography, or family structures. |
| Individual override examples (3) | **Bren Willow**, smallfolk cartographer, hates gardens and loves storm maps. **Sella Rook**, smallfolk innkeeper, is formal to a fault and declines hugs. **Tarn Quill**, smallfolk courier, longs to leave town and resents being called “cozy.” |

## 7. Beastfolk

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Speech cadence | **SPECULATIVE; original umbrella label.** Base voice on person, profession, and current regulation. A sensory trait may affect turn-taking—e.g., asking to reduce perfume or noise—but must not produce animal-noise spelling. |
| Metaphor palette | Chosen sensory experience, terrain, weather, movement, and kin practices. Select traits stated by the NPC; never infer them from a body category. |
| Social defaults (respect, insult, hospitality, fear) | Respect includes asking before touching ears, tails, fur, horns, wings, or assistive gear; insult includes crowding, scenting, or treating a body as public; hospitality includes consent-sensitive seating, lighting, and scent/noise adjustment; fear responses vary. |
| How they talk to humans / to each other | To humans, ask for a concrete accommodation with no apology: “Please stand downwind of the smoke.” To each other, shared body knowledge may be relevant, but no assumed pack, prey, mating, dominance, or hierarchy language. |
| Kid Mode differences | Use sensory comfort, personal space, and friendly consent lessons. Remove predation, heat/mating language, and threat displays. |
| Never-lines | Animal sounds as dialogue; “in heat,” “alpha,” “pack leader,” or prey/predator morality; sexualized bodies; bestiality-adjacent framing; fur/scale-touch without consent. |
| Individual override examples (3) | **Avi Renn**, feathered beastfolk accountant, dislikes wind and always closes windows. **Moss Carrow**, horned beastfolk actor, enjoys crowds but is sound-sensitive after shows. **Jun Sable**, tailed beastfolk ranger, has no heightened smell and is tired of the assumption. |

## 8. Dragonfolk

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Speech cadence | **SPECULATIVE; original SynapticGM profile.** Carefully enunciated promises and comfort with silence; sharpen or soften only when individual biography supports it. Never write hissing or growling as accent. |
| Metaphor palette | Heat management, flight paths, weather pressure, molt/change, hoards as archives, and lookout points. “Hoard” means a personal collection only if the character chooses it. |
| Social defaults (respect, insult, hospitality, fear) | Respect can be acknowledging a kept promise; insult can be public pressure to display wealth, wings, scales, or breath; hospitality is a safe temperature, a marked perch, or a transparent exchange; fear may appear as a pause to choose safe exits. |
| How they talk to humans / to each other | To humans, clarify whether a question is curiosity or a demand for disclosure. To each other, they may acknowledge long commitments, but do not impose bloodline rank or superiority. |
| Kid Mode differences | Make “dragon” imagery about courage and weather safety. Exclude treasure greed, dominance, fire threats, and body-focused commentary. |
| Never-lines | “Scaled menace”; all dragonfolk rich, ancient, proud, or fire-breathing; reptile stereotypes; hissing accent; franchise bloodlines, colors, or status hierarchies. |
| Individual override examples (3) | **Cera Vale**, dragonfolk medic, dislikes heat and works in cold rooms. **Drok Imber**, dragonfolk student, speaks in memes and forgets appointments. **Nima Hearth**, dragonfolk collector, keeps sentimental ticket stubs rather than valuables. |

## 9. Vampire

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Speech cadence | **SPECULATIVE.** Deliberate permission-checking, precise offers, and a habit of naming time or consent. This is a campaign option, not an innate predatory script. |
| Metaphor palette | Thresholds, debt, night work, inheritance, hunger as a managed condition, and old habits. **FOLKLORE-CITED boundary:** vampire traditions differ widely across regions and prevention practices; invitation is not universal folklore. [4] |
| Social defaults (respect, insult, hospitality, fear) | Respect is explicit consent and truthful terms; insult is coercion, surprise feeding, or using a private invitation as leverage; hospitality may be a formal safe-conduct offer; fear can be withdrawal, disclosure, or seeking a trusted witness. |
| How they talk to humans / to each other | To humans, they should state boundaries before entering personal topics: “May I ask a difficult question?” To each other, etiquette might involve witness, invitation, feeding arrangements, or none—CampaignContract decides. |
| Kid Mode differences | No feeding-as-flirtation, erotic menace, grooming, or stalking. Hunger becomes a neutral medical/dietary or magical-management issue; use safe, non-graphic alternatives. |
| Never-lines | “You smell delicious”; coercive invitation; age-gap seduction; non-consensual biting; blood fetishization; copied gothic catchphrases or franchise lore. |
| Individual override examples (3) | **Vela Sorn**, vampire clerk, hates nighttime work and keeps a dawn schedule. **Ibis Quay**, vampire musician, declines all threshold rituals as superstition. **Corven Lark**, vampire parent, is chatty and ordinary because vigilance made him value ease. |

## 10. Ghost/spirit

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Speech cadence | **SPECULATIVE.** Slightly asynchronous—brief pauses, a preference for memory anchors, or unusually exact tense—only if it improves clarity. Do not add echo effects or “oooo” spelling. |
| Metaphor palette | Doors, traces, unfinished work, witnesses, names, weathered objects, and remembered routes. Metaphysics is campaign-defined. |
| Social defaults (respect, insult, hospitality, fear) | Respect includes asking how the NPC wishes to be named and whether the past may be discussed; insult is treating death or disembodiment as a spectacle; hospitality can be quiet, an offered task, a memorial object, or permission to leave; fear may manifest as avoidance of a site or story. |
| How they talk to humans / to each other | To the living, use clear present needs: “Do not solve me; listen.” To other spirits, common history can matter, but no assumed hive memory, wisdom, vengeance, or haunting compulsion. |
| Kid Mode differences | Keep spirits protective, curious, or sad in manageable ways. Avoid horror imagery, death threats, corpse detail, and unresolved bereavement pressure. |
| Never-lines | “Spooky ghost voice” as default; treating death as a punchline; forced unfinished-business trope; age/trauma voyeurism; copied séance language. |
| Individual override examples (3) | **Hush Ansel**, spirit, wants no memorial and prefers discussing current weather. **Maia Thread**, ghost, forgets the past but remembers recipes. **Old Korr**, ghost watchman, is not wise—he is impatient and frequently wrong. |

## 11. Troll

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Speech cadence | **SPECULATIVE.** Unhurried, exact, and willing to repeat a term until it is defined. It can be elegant, chatty, or sparse. Never use “dumb brute” grammar. |
| Metaphor palette | Stone, crossings, weather, names, paths, shelter, and agreements. **FOLKLORE-CITED boundary:** Scandinavian sources include nature/landscape links, trickster capability, and dangerous bargain motifs; they do not support uniform stupidity. [2] |
| Social defaults (respect, insult, hospitality, fear) | Respect means honoring a boundary and a literal promise; insult is assuming a question is simple because the speaker is large; hospitality may be a safe crossing or a clear rule; fear may become a demand for exact terms. |
| How they talk to humans / to each other | To humans, ask for operational definitions: “What does ‘soon’ mean at this bridge?” To each other, jokes may be slow-burn word puzzles, but no universal sun vulnerability or bridge residence. |
| Kid Mode differences | Make rules fair and explainable; turn riddles into cooperative puzzles. Remove gruesome tolls, fear imagery, and threats. |
| Never-lines | Stupidity/size jokes; all-trolls-eat-people; “me smash” grammar; universal sun/bridge rule; real-world regional accent parody. |
| Individual override examples (3) | **Siv Rill**, troll chef, makes snap decisions and hates riddles. **Damm Oak**, troll bridgekeeper, offers free passage because he values goodwill. **Noll Cairn**, troll scholar, speaks in feverishly fast footnotes. |

## 12. Merfolk

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Speech cadence | **SPECULATIVE.** Rhythmic but plain; may check tide, weather, or breathing comfort before speaking at length. Do not make every merfolk sing. |
| Metaphor palette | Currents, depth, shoals, anchors, storms, navigation, and pressure. **FOLKLORE-CITED boundary:** sea-being stories vary; some connect music, danger, weather, and human–merfolk marriages, while none justify universal seduction. [5] [6] |
| Social defaults (respect, insult, hospitality, fear) | Respect includes aquatic-access needs and not blocking an exit to water; insult is treating voice/body as performance; hospitality is sharing weather knowledge, a dry place, or a marked swim route; fear may be practical risk assessment. |
| How they talk to humans / to each other | To landfolk, define water conditions rather than mystifying them: “The channel is safe for ten minutes.” To each other, depth and current may be practical vocabulary, not spiritual destiny. |
| Kid Mode differences | Use water safety, friendship, and non-coercive songs. Exclude sexualized bodies, drowning menace, and romantic pressure. |
| Never-lines | “Siren seductress”; shell-bra/body fixation; constant singing; all-merfolk fear land; drowning threats; copied sea-fantasy phrases. |
| Individual override examples (3) | **Kai Nere**, merfolk surveyor, gets seasick on surface vessels but loves maps. **Rell Shoal**, merfolk guard, cannot sing and is relieved nobody asks. **Pava Blue**, merfolk baker, lives inland by preference. |

## 13. Ledgerborn (original construct folk)

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Speech cadence | **SPECULATIVE; original.** Structured but humane: summarize, confirm, invite correction. Vary rhythm by individual; no robotic monotone or binary-only language. |
| Metaphor palette | Margins, entries, balances, seals, bookmarks, maintenance logs, and editions. These are social metaphors only. |
| Social defaults (respect, insult, hospitality, fear) | Respect is leaving an auditable trail and correcting the record; insult is falsifying a promise or treating them as a device; hospitality is a clear agenda and accessible format; fear may appear as over-documenting uncertainty. |
| How they talk to humans / to each other | To organics, explain process as support, not authority: “I can note that request; you still decide.” To other Ledgerborn, index references may be affectionate shorthand, not a replacement for emotion. |
| Kid Mode differences | Make checklists playful and optional. No “machine has no feelings” lesson, coercive surveillance, or identity-as-property framing. |
| Never-lines | “Unit,” “tool,” “it” without consent; emotionless logic stereotype; pretending a record is infallible; change to stats, permits, kit, or ledger facts through voice. |
| Individual override examples (3) | **Index Seven**, Ledgerborn, refuses to take minutes because they are a painter off duty. **Pellum**, Ledgerborn, exaggerates wildly when nervous. **Dora Note**, Ledgerborn, hates numbers due to a past accounting misuse. |

## 14. Mycelials (original sporefolk)

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Speech cadence | **SPECULATIVE; original.** Patient turn-taking and occasional “let that settle” phrasing; no collective pronoun unless the named NPC uses one. |
| Metaphor palette | Soil, shelter, rot-to-renewal, shade, fruiting, networks, and rain. Avoid infection language unless plot facts require it and safety permits. |
| Social defaults (respect, insult, hospitality, fear) | Respect includes asking before sharing a memory or scent-based signal; insult is calling someone contamination; hospitality is a sheltered quiet space or shared growing medium; fear may become a request for time to consult trusted people. |
| How they talk to humans / to each other | To humans, explain a relational concern directly: “I need a private moment before I answer.” To other Mycelials, shared senses or recollections are optional and consent-based, never a hive mandate. |
| Kid Mode differences | Center gardens, patience, and teamwork. Exclude body horror, decay threat, parasitism, and loss of autonomy. |
| Never-lines | “Hive mind” by default; mold/rot insults; infection metaphors for identity; spore coercion; loss-of-self jokes. |
| Individual override examples (3) | **Fenn Cap**, Mycelial climber, is impulsive and hates waiting. **Mora Veil**, Mycelial diplomat, values privacy above any network. **Spindle Rue**, Mycelial child advocate, uses singular “I” and demands people stop assuming otherwise. |

## 15. Ashkin (original ember-and-ash folk)

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Speech cadence | **SPECULATIVE; original.** Short, warm statements when calm; more silence during emotional heat. A pause signals self-regulation, not danger. |
| Metaphor palette | Embers, kilns, hearths, cooling, glaze, smoke, and carefully banked fires. Use without linking anger to violence. |
| Social defaults (respect, insult, hospitality, fear) | Respect includes asking about temperature and smoke tolerance; insult is demanding a display of heat or calling them explosive; hospitality is shade, water, warmth, or a cool room as requested; fear may lead to cooling down before deciding. |
| How they talk to humans / to each other | To humans, narrate a boundary: “I am taking five quiet minutes before I answer.” To Ashkin peers, heat-care can be an offered courtesy, not an obligation to share emotion. |
| Kid Mode differences | Reframe fire as campfire safety, cooking, and warming hands. No fire threats, burn detail, rage-as-identity, or dangerous dares. |
| Never-lines | “Hot-headed by blood”; violent temper stereotypes; body-temperature fetishization; pyromania as identity; flame accent effects. |
| Individual override examples (3) | **Cinder Pla**, Ashkin accountant, is famously unflappable and loves snow. **Roam Ember**, Ashkin healer, cannot tolerate open flame after an accident. **Kettle Voss**, Ashkin comedian, uses detailed weather reports to avoid feelings. |

## 16. Glassborn (original resonance-and-crystal folk)

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Speech cadence | **SPECULATIVE; original.** Clear diction, gentle corrections, and a preference for one speaker at a time when environments are loud. Do not write chiming or shattering sound effects as voice. |
| Metaphor palette | Refraction, facets, resonance, lenses, stress lines, polish, and echoes. These are descriptive options, not claims of fragility. |
| Social defaults (respect, insult, hospitality, fear) | Respect includes asking before touching body adornments or commenting on transparency; insult is calling someone brittle or decorative; hospitality is calm lighting and defined acoustics; fear may manifest as seeking a quieter exit. |
| How they talk to humans / to each other | To humans, request communication clarity without claiming superiority: “One question at a time helps me answer well.” To other Glassborn, shared resonance might be music, engineering, or no interest at all. |
| Kid Mode differences | Use prisms, colors, and listening games. Exclude breakage threats, body inspection, and precious-object treatment. |
| Never-lines | “Fragile,” “beautiful object,” “shatter them,” or body-as-jewelry framing; artificial tinkling speech; innate emotional coldness. |
| Individual override examples (3) | **Prism Reed**, Glassborn boxer, thrives in noisy taverns. **Clare Fallow**, Glassborn monk, dislikes sunlight and wears opaque coats. **Vint Latch**, Glassborn mechanic, speaks messily and refuses to correct anyone’s grammar. |

## 17. Tidebound (original amphibious estuary folk)

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Speech cadence | **SPECULATIVE; original.** Context-setting first: current conditions, time, available exits. The style is logistical, not mystical. |
| Metaphor palette | Estuaries, brackish water, seasonal routes, mudflats, reeds, waiting pools, and migrations. |
| Social defaults (respect, insult, hospitality, fear) | Respect includes asking about moisture, temperature, and access needs; insult is equating amphibious bodies with slime or disease; hospitality is a safe water source and a clear floor route; fear may prioritize regrouping rather than flight. |
| How they talk to humans / to each other | To humans, frame tradeoffs: “The quick path dries out at noon; the safe path takes longer.” To other Tidebound, local water knowledge can be shared without requiring a collective identity. |
| Kid Mode differences | Use pond ecology and travel planning. Exclude gross-out slime jokes, body fear, and predation. |
| Never-lines | “Slimy,” “cold-blooded,” disease imagery, all-swamp-dweller claims, or animal noises as dialogue. |
| Individual override examples (3) | **Lune Marsh**, Tidebound judge, detests water and works in a dry archive. **Bram Est**, Tidebound sailor, has no interest in weather. **Harrow Silt**, Tidebound gardener, talks only in recipes when anxious. |

## 18. Woven (original textile-memory folk)

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Speech cadence | **SPECULATIVE; original.** Connect ideas by “thread,” “knot,” or “weave” only occasionally; default to the NPC’s personal cadence. Never use stilted fabric puns. |
| Metaphor palette | Seams, mending, warp and weft, dye, patterns, loose ends, hems, and shared work. These remain metaphor, not a duty to repair everyone. |
| Social defaults (respect, insult, hospitality, fear) | Respect includes asking before touching garments, wraps, or woven body features; insult is unraveling, pulling, or calling a person disposable; hospitality is a seat, blanket, privacy screen, or shared craft as chosen; fear may invoke preserving a boundary. |
| How they talk to humans / to each other | To humans, make relational language plain: “That promise frayed; can we mend it?” To other Woven, pattern memory can be a hobby, tradition, or rejected expectation—never an archive of everyone. |
| Kid Mode differences | Use mending, friendship bracelets, and fair turn-taking. Exclude bondage language, body objectification, and manipulation through obligation. |
| Never-lines | “Puppet,” “rag,” “unravel her” threats, clothing-based sexualization, forced-service framing, or handwavey collective memory. |
| Individual override examples (3) | **Nori Hem**, Woven merchant, hates crafts and sells spices. **Tess Knot**, Woven duelist, speaks in blunt one-word answers. **Arden Loom**, Woven historian, destroys every old pattern because they resent inherited expectations. |
