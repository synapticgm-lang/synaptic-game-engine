# Kid Mode and Plain-Language Research Notes

## VERIFIED findings

W3C supplemental cognitive-accessibility guidance recommends easy-to-understand words, short sentences, simple tense, short text blocks, unambiguous content, clear images, literal language, separated instructions, and familiar conventions. It also emphasizes helping users avoid mistakes and making correction easy. Sources: [W3C — Use Clear and Understandable Content](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/objectives/o3-clear-content/) and [Making Content Usable for People with Cognitive and Learning Disabilities](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/).

Digital.gov defines plain language as content that is clear and easy to understand for its specific audience and treats writing, design, and testing as a combined practice. Source: [Digital.gov Plain Language Guide Series](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language).

## SynapticGM application

Kid Mode should use direct subject–verb–object sentences, common words, one instruction per line, explicit consequence wording, and a visible safe next step. It must not hide ledger loss, but it can replace graphic detail with non-graphic consequence language. It should disable coercive urgency, sexual/adult chrome, mockery, dense metaphor, nested negation, and ambiguous consent. Tone remains distinct through rhythm and harmless sensory cues rather than adult vocabulary or intensified peril.

Choice pads in Kid Mode should state the action and likely immediate aim without promising an outcome. Repair copy should say what failed, what stayed unchanged, and what the player can try next.
