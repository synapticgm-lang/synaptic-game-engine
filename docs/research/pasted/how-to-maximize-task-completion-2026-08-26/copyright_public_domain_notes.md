# Copyright and Public-Domain Boundary Notes

## VERIFIED

The U.S. Copyright Office states that copyright duration depends on factors including publication status and date; for most works created after 1978, the general term is the author’s life plus 70 years, with different rules for anonymous, pseudonymous, and works made for hire. It also explains that when copyright expires, a work enters the public domain and that ideas, facts, titles, discoveries, and procedures are not protected by copyright, although trademark or patent law can still apply. Sources: [Copyright Duration FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-duration.html) and [The Lifecycle of Copyright](https://www.copyright.gov/history/copyright-exhibit/lifecycle/).

Project Gutenberg’s license explains that most individual works in its collection are unrestricted by U.S. copyright law, but some are included with permission, its trademark and license text have separate rules, and users outside the United States must check local law. Source: [Project Gutenberg License](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/license.html).

## SynapticGM application

Public-domain works may support **technique analysis**, but release teams should not assume worldwide clearance from a U.S.-only catalogue status. Product banks should be original and should not copy source passages, distinctive modern franchises, living-author voices, trademarks, logos, or studio aesthetics. Any commercial seasonal bundle, geographically broad release, or use of source-derived names and visual trade dress is marked **COUNSEL**.
