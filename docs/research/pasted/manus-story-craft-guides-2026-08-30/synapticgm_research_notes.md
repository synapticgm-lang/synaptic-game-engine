# SynapticGM Story-Craft Research Notes

## PYOA / choice-based fiction

### Sam Kabo Ashwell — “Standard Patterns in Choice-Based Games” (2015)
URL: https://heterogenoustasks.wordpress.com/2015/01/26/standard-patterns-in-choice-based-games/

Verified points: Ashwell distinguishes Time Cave, Gauntlet, and Branch-and-Bottleneck structures. Branch-and-bottleneck relies on state tracking so rejoining does not erase prior choices; early choices can accumulate and produce later divergence. Gauntlets tend to privilege one path and prune failures, so they risk fake agency if overused. Cite-only/paraphrase under ordinary copyright.

### Choice of Games — “By the Numbers: How to Write a Long Interactive Novel That Doesn’t Suck” (2011)
URL: https://www.choiceofgames.com/2011/07/by-the-numbers-how-to-write-a-long-interactive-novel-that-doesnt-suck/

Page identity and authoring context verified. Search extract states that numeric state can make a choice consequential because the decision changes a stat, providing a durable effect even when branches later rejoin. Full textual extraction still needed. Cite-only/paraphrase unless the page states otherwise.

## Tabletop fantasy GM craft

### Justin Alexander — “Don’t Prep Plots” (2009; page updated/published archive shows 2011 metadata)
URL: https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/4147/roleplaying-games/dont-prep-plots

Verified points: A plot predetermines events; a situation supplies circumstances whose outcomes depend on player action. Plot chains create failure points and pressure the GM to railroad. Situation prep is more robust. For chokepoints, multiple viable routes prevent a single failed check or refused action from halting play. Cite-only/paraphrase.

### John Harper / Evil Hat — Blades in the Dark SRD, “The Basics”
URL: https://bladesinthedark.com/basics

Verified points: Players and GM are co-authors of tone, mechanics, and consequences. The GM establishes a dynamic world, gives each NPC a concrete desire and preferred method, presents opportunities, and follows the chain of action and consequences rather than planning events. The page explicitly says the GM is not in charge of the story. SRD; licensing page must be cited for precise reuse terms.

## Story / narrative RPG craft

### Aaron Marks — “Powered by the Apocalypse 201” (2018)
URL: https://cannibalhalflinggaming.com/2018/11/21/level-one-wonk-pbta-201/

Verified points: Debts and Strings are relationship-state tokens accumulated through setup and play. They can be spent to compel action, honor obligations, or express influence/social capital. Adaptation lesson: surface existing leverage as pressure that changes available futures; do not convert every social exchange into a generic persuasion check. Cite-only/paraphrase.

### Fate Core SRD — “Invoking & Compelling Aspects”
URL: https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/invoking-compelling-aspects

Verified page identity and explicit CC BY 3.0 license. Compels are a public SRD model for turning an established character or situation aspect into a complication. The player can negotiate or refuse, which preserves player authorship; SynapticGM should offer pressure and consequences rather than assert the PC’s inner feelings or decisions. Freely quotable with attribution under the stated license.

## LitRPG / progression craft

### Andrew Rowe — “Writing Progression Fantasy” (2019)
URL: https://andrewkrowe.wordpress.com/2019/03/02/writing-progression-fantasy/

Verified points: Advancement is most satisfying when earned through cleverness, effort, survival, or prepared character development. Arbitrary increases undermine believability; perfectly even progression can become dull. Emotional breakthroughs need prior setup to avoid feeling unearned. Cite-only/paraphrase.

URL: https://ninc.com/litrpg-a-genre-spotlight/

The article emphasizes coherent systems, visible progression, meaningful stakes, and balancing advancement speed with story coherence. Cite-only/paraphrase.

## Cross-mode interactive-fiction craft

### Emily Short — “Conversation” (living essay/index)
URL: https://emshort.blog/how-to-play/writing-if/my-articles/conversation/

Verified points: When a player repeats a question, the text can summarize remembered information, vary delivery, or let the NPC react; however, essential information must remain recoverable. Conversation state and current topic can change the interpretation and available reactions. Adaptation lesson: do not replay an NPC’s first speech verbatim; either provide a compact restatement when asked or advance the NPC’s tactic, mood, demand, or disclosure. Cite-only/paraphrase.

### Emily Short — “Plotting for Interactivity: The Set-Piece or Crisis” (2011)
URL: https://emshort.blog/2011/07/14/plotting-for-interactivity-the-set-piece-or-crisis/

Page identity verified; full extraction needs a text-only pass because browser markdown did not surface the body. Retain as a relevant cite-only source, but ground detailed claims in the extracted version before final use.

### Royal Road community guide — “LitRPG Guide: What Makes a Good LitRPG” (2020)
URL: https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/97990

Verified points: This community critique argues that stats weaken the work when they do not affect plot, that numerical escalation can destroy challenge, and that leveling is not a substitute for character development. It contains licensed-example references and coarse language, so only the abstract craft lessons should be paraphrased. Cite-only/community evidence.

### Bluelightning42 — “Guide: How to Make a LitRPG [System]” (2022)
URL: https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/120806

Verified points: System components need a purpose in the world; rewards should follow the behaviors the system is designed to encourage; default kill-XP can feel copied-on if it contradicts the system’s premise; progression rate and reward types should follow from function and setting. Cite-only/community guidance.

### J. D. Mitchell — “How I Wrote a Gamebook: The Basics” (2022)
URL: https://jdmitchellwriter.com/2022/04/11/how-i-wrote-a-gamebook-the-basics/

Verified points: Rules should be plain and concise; entries should be compact enough to preserve flow; tests should maintain a sense of danger while still allowing progress; repeated forced restarts are not enjoyable. Cite-only/paraphrase.

### Lloyd of Gamebooks — “Gamebook Mechanics: Meaningful Choices” (2017)
URL: https://www.lloydofgamebooks.com/2017/11/gamebook-mechanics-meaningful-choices_9.html

Page identity and author metadata verified. The source frames meaningful choice as a balance between too little information (arbitrary door-choice) and over-obvious selection. Full extraction was limited; retain as cite-only and use only high-level claims.

### ChooseCo omission note
The official Choose Your Own Adventure site establishes the second-person reader-as-hero format but does not expose a substantial freely licensed writing-craft manual in the public search results. The guide should therefore justify omitting direct ChooseCo craft rules and use Choice of Games, Ashwell, Short, Dias, and public gamebook essays as citable functional equivalents. “Choose Your Own Adventure” is also a trademarked brand, so SynapticGM should prefer the mode name PYOA and generic terms such as choice-based fiction or gamebook.
