# SynapticGM — Folklore Appendix

**Author:** Manus AI

This appendix documents the **public-domain motif layer** used to inspire original pointer cards. Harvard Library defines a motif as “the smallest definite element of a tale” and describes motif indexes as tools for comparing recurring narrative building blocks across different tales.[1] Indiana University Libraries likewise characterizes type and motif indexes as tools for locating variants of folk-literature texts.[2] The catalogue therefore borrows **motif shapes**, not plots, prose, characters, or modern franchise identity.

> “A motif is the smallest definite element of a tale.” — Harvard Library research guide[1]

The classic Thompson classification includes broad families such as mythological motifs, magic, the dead, marvels, ordeals, deceptions, reversals of fortune, fate, society, rewards, and punishments.[1] [3] Harvard’s guide also cautions that the classic index is geographically limited and points to later cultural and regional indexes; SynapticGM should therefore avoid presenting any one classification as universal.[1]

## Motifs and originalisation

| Public-domain motif or folklore name | Traditional shape used | SynapticGM originalisation |
|---|---|---|
| **Summoning circle** | A marked boundary separates ordinary space from an invoked presence. | The circle is treated as damaged civic or ritual infrastructure whose witnesses disagree about responsibility; it never supplies a borrowed cosmology. |
| **Threshold taboo** | A door, bridge, gate, or boundary changes what may cross and under what condition. | Thresholds become explicit causal rules with testable exceptions, appeal paths, and visible consequences rather than arbitrary denial. |
| **Crossroads choice** | Intersecting roads externalize uncertainty, encounter, and altered fate. | The catalogue uses crossroads as route pressure: each branch changes custody, time, witnesses, or resources instead of merely signaling destiny. |
| **Bargain and promise** | A benefit is paired with a duty, deadline, pledge, or hidden interpretation. | SynapticGM states all player-perceivable terms, preserves refusal, and uses later causal enforcement rather than a prewritten trick ending. |
| **Deceptive bargain** | The wording or apparent value of an exchange conceals an adverse meaning. | Deception is assigned to an interested actor and made discoverable through evidence; the engine never hides information the player should reasonably perceive. |
| **Geas or binding obligation** | A promise, prohibition, or charged duty constrains later conduct. | The obligation is narrowed to an explicit clause, attached to witnesses or a record, and supplied with amendment, appeal, breach, or release paths. |
| **Psychopomp** | A guide conducts a traveler across a boundary associated with death or another realm. | The guide becomes an independent companion with a known stopping point and no ownership over the traveler’s final choice. |
| **Ferryman** | Passage requires payment, permission, memory, or another condition. | Ferry beats become custody and access negotiations involving who travels, who waits, and what public crossing rules should become. |
| **Inn as hearth** | A shared fire and meal create temporary hospitality among strangers. | Inns are original working spaces—kitchen, ledger desk, drying room, stable, or common table—where labor and rumor cross without making the hub perfectly safe. |
| **Guest-right and host-duty** | Host and guest receive reciprocal protection and obligations. | Hospitality is written as bounded, visible procedure: weapons lowered, food shared, names optional, and violations carrying social rather than automatic scripted outcomes. |
| **Guild as work association** | Skilled workers regulate training, access, reputation, and obligations. | Guilds become plural institutions with contracts, apprentices, public duties, and class tensions; no branded rules terms or famous setting structures are imported. |
| **Job board or public notice** | Posted work makes private need legible to a wider community. | Notices are treated as incomplete claims that can be questioned, corrected, ignored, or linked to who benefits from the task. |
| **Living book or speaking record** | A text responds, remembers, reveals, or judges. | Records preserve custody, witness, revision, and consequence; they never provide omniscient exposition or reproduce a copyrighted interface voice. |
| **Transformation ordeal** | A changed body or form tests identity, loyalty, restraint, or adaptation. | Transformation is framed as a capability with permanent tradeoffs and bodily limits, not a cosmetic reward or imitation of a modern character design. |
| **Trial by task** | A claimant proves fitness through a sequence of difficult acts. | Trials test a family-specific value—care, proof, law comprehension, repair, restraint, or teamwork—rather than raw strength alone. |
| **Prohibition or taboo** | A stated action must not be taken, often with consequences for violation. | The prohibition’s scope and source are made observable; players may obey, test, appeal, or deliberately breach it and see the world respond consistently. |
| **Supernatural helper** | A strange guide, animal, ancestor, or spirit provides aid after recognition or kindness. | Helpers retain goals, limits, and the right to leave; help is never a blank authorization for the GM to solve the player’s problem. |
| **Household spirit** | A presence protects or troubles a home according to custom, neglect, or exchange. | The shape becomes maintenance pressure, remembered labor, and negotiated residence without importing a named modern creature or fixed bestiary entry. |
| **Revenant or restless dead** | The dead return because an oath, burial, injury, or truth remains unresolved. | Hauntings are tied to recoverable obligations and multiple remedies—memorial, restitution, testimony, containment, or combat when necessary. |
| **Vampiric revenant** | A predatory dead or night-being is constrained by hunger, threshold custom, invitation, daylight, or protective practice. | Crimson Nocturne uses original factions, ampoule custody, explicit restraint, and public-domain limits; it does not imitate any modern vampire romance or action setting. |
| **Salt as boundary or protection** | Salt marks preservation, value, purity, wage, hospitality, or a defensive line in various traditions. | Salt becomes trade infrastructure, ration pressure, road taxation, food preservation, or a visible boundary whose cost and labor remain part of play. |
| **Sea oath and sailor custom** | Voyages accumulate rules, omens, promises, and debts under uncertain weather. | Maritime cards connect custom to watch schedules, repairs, crew authority, pressure, salvage, and whether knowledge is shared rather than to a borrowed nautical mythos. |
| **Sea bargain** | Passage, rescue, transformation, or safe return is exchanged for a future price. | The exchange is written with stated dosage, custody, deadline, and refusal alternatives; the sea remains an environment with physical consequences. |
| **Forbidden chamber** | A room is closed by warning, secrecy, law, or danger, and opening it changes the household. | The chamber is an evidence and consent problem: who may enter, what cause is preserved, and which private fact is relevant to the campaign. |
| **Manor murder** | A bounded household, limited suspects, private motives, and physical clues structure inquiry. | Giltwood varies camera, witness posture, and first proof while keeping culprit logic stable; secrecy is never treated as automatic guilt. |
| **Locked-room puzzle** | An apparently sealed scene challenges the investigator to reconstruct means, timing, and access. | The solution must remain physically causal and compatible with preserved evidence; supernatural explanation cannot replace reconstruction in the mystery family. |
| **Backward or stopped timepiece** | A damaged clock or watch appears to fix, distort, or contradict a timeline. | The backward watch is a custody-bearing clue whose meaning changes through comparison with routes, witness access, and other physical evidence. |
| **Mistaken room or wrong threshold** | A traveler enters a space not intended for them and gains dangerous knowledge or status. | The camera creates a temporary information imbalance; it never silently rewrites identity, faction, or consent. |
| **Rival arrives first** | Another claimant reaches the object, witness, or place moments earlier. | The rival’s move creates a persistent obstacle but cannot end the player’s agency; pursuit, negotiation, exposure, and alternate routes remain available. |
| **Name taboo or true-name power** | A name may confer recognition, authority, vulnerability, kinship, or obligation. | The catalogue always allows a chosen name, alias, title, blank register, delayed answer, or refusal unless the player has knowingly accepted a narrow oath. |
| **Hospitality test** | A stranger’s treatment reveals a household or ruler’s character. | The test becomes reciprocal and nonbinary: limited resources, staff labor, boundaries, and later consequences matter more than a hidden morality score. |
| **Fate ledger or book of lives** | A written record appears to assign relationship, destiny, debt, or death. | Ledgers are contestable social artifacts with custodians, errors, amendments, and opt-out endings; no entry overrides adult consent or player identity. |
| **Rebirth bargain** | A traveler receives a new life, body, duty, or gift under conditions. | Rebirth cards separate Flaw, Boon, body, memory, destination, and appeal so the player can negotiate rather than receive a scripted destiny. |
| **Return with foreknowledge** | A person repeats an earlier span of life while remembering some prior events. | Foreknowledge is fallible evidence degraded by butterfly effects; each intervention changes later reliability instead of guaranteeing optimal play. |
| **Safe circle or sanctuary** | A bounded place suspends hostility under custom, oath, or protection. | Safety is local and finite: food, law, time, rumor, debt, heat, oxygen, or pursuit can still matter, preventing hubs from erasing the campaign. |
| **Meal after danger** | Shared food marks recovery, reconciliation, hierarchy, or unresolved strain. | The meal is a working social scene with preparation, scarcity, seating, service labor, and optional conversation—not a mandatory sentimental reset. |

## Usage rules

| Rule | Application |
|---|---|
| Motif, not script | Use the traditional shape as one ingredient and write new prose, causal details, people, and stakes. |
| No modern comparator | Do not name a licensed series, tabletop brand, studio, publisher, or character as a shortcut for tone or structure. |
| Cultural humility | Do not claim that a motif is universal merely because it appears in a classic index; consult tradition-specific sources before adding culturally specific ritual detail.[1] [2] |
| Public-domain naming only | Traditional figures or terms may be named for research context, but live cards should prefer original functional wording unless the cultural name is necessary and respectfully used. |
| Player agency | Every bargain, name request, companion posture, transformation, romance beat, and rebirth condition must preserve meaningful acceptance, refusal, or revision. |
| Causal proof | A motif becomes game-ready only when player action changes an object, resource, route, witness, relationship, rule, or later event. |
| Kid Mode | Soften fear, injury, coercion, vice, and adult intimacy without making consent or consequence disappear. |
