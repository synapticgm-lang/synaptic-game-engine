# Folklore Research Notes

## Verified foundations

Harvard Library defines a **motif** as “the smallest definite element of a tale” and explains that motif indexes identify recurring building blocks across different narratives. The guide also cautions that Thompson’s classic index is geographically limited, principally toward Europe, and notes the importance of later regional and cultural indexes. This supports using motifs as modular ingredients rather than treating any one plot as a template.

Indiana University Libraries describes type and motif indexes as tools for locating variants of folk-literature texts. Its bibliography includes the international tale-type index as well as region-specific collections, reinforcing the need to treat folklore as plural and culturally situated rather than universal or property-like.

The online Thompson motif classification lists traditional categories including deceptive bargains, bargains and promises, ordeals, prohibitions, supernatural helpers, the dead, fate, and society. These categories are useful as high-level sources for original pointer-card ingredients, but wording and combinations must be newly written for SynapticGM.

## Originalization policy derived from the research

The appendix will name traditional motif families only when they are public-domain cultural material or scholarly classification terms. Every game-facing use will be reframed as a new causal ingredient, social pressure, sensory camera, or consequence. No traditional tale will be reproduced as a script, and no modern franchise title will be used as a comparator.
