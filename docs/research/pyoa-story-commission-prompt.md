# Commission prompt: Pick Your Own Adventure bible

Paste this into another AI when you want a **new** SynapticGM PYOA campaign. We ingest the output as a campaign bible (like Thornferry Road). Original worlds only — no licensed plots, places, or character names.

---

You are writing a **pick-your-own-adventure campaign bible** for SynapticGM, not a novel and not an open-world game.

## What this engine actually does
- Code owns facts (inventory, who is with you, what you already chose).
- The writer only writes **this turn’s camera**: 2–6 sentences, then 3–4 numbered choices.
- The player’s typed **comments and reactions** are the main character’s inner voice. They can be good or cruel, loyal or treacherous.
- There is a **clear main spine**. Side work is optional and hidden until the player looks, talks, or wanders.
- Less open world than a sandbox RPG. More main story, with forks.

## Do not copy
No Fable / Albion / Heroes Guild, no named novels, anime, or games. Genre tropes only. Invent original town, road, NPCs, and MacGuffin.

## Write all of the following

### 1. Pitch
- Title (3 words or fewer if possible)
- Tagline (one sentence)
- Starting location (a real place in the world, never the title)
- Tone options the player’s comments can pull: wry / earnest / dark

### 2. Main spine (7 beats, skeleton only)
Numbered 1–7. Unique prose each run; do not lecture this list in play.
Beat 1 must be a first meeting and a party-or-solo offer.
Somewhere in 2–4: a trust test (ally vs betray a named person).
Beat 7 is the spine’s resolution (deliver / keep / sell / burn / forge the MacGuffin, or the equivalent).

### 3. Relationship stamps (must be playable)
- **Party vs solo**: one named companion who can walk with you, leave, or become a rival.
- **Ally vs betray**: at least two factions or people who both want something from you.
- Stamps **stick**. NPCs remember. No alignment meter speech.

### 4. Inner voice
State that player comments, jokes, and doubts are the hero thinking or speaking. The GM mirrors them, then the world answers.

### 5. Side quest seeds (5–8)
Hidden until earned. One line each. Never dump the list at the opening.

### 6. Several endings (at least 6)
Each ending is a combination of stamps, for example:
- Companion stayed + you kept the thing local
- Companion stayed + you sold them out
- Solo + kept
- Solo + sold
- You destroyed or forged the thing
- You delivered it honestly
Do **not** name endings in play. The GM only plays toward one after beat 7.

### 7. Opening kit
Named clothes / one quest object. No generic iron shortsword unless the story is a soldier start.

### 8. Key NPCs (3–5)
Name, role, disposition (friendly / neutral / hostile / ambiguous), what they want, how they react to betrayal.

### 9. License note
One line: original setting, tropes only, not based on any named series.

## Output format
Use this exact shape so we can paste it into a campaign file:

```
TITLE:
TAGLINE:
STARTING LOCATION:
OPENING HOOK: (one paragraph the player hears before name/place)

PREMISE: (binding rails first: inner voice, ally/betray, party/solo, spine, sides, endings)

LORE: (4–6 snippets: world, MacGuffin, factions, “walking together”)

NPCS: (3–5)

STARTER QUEST: (one main quest; reveal only after the opening scene)

STARTER ITEM: (the MacGuffin)

ENDINGS: (6+, keyed to stamps)
```

Keep it compact enough to fit in a premise block (~1,500–2,000 words total). Unique names. No licensed IP.
