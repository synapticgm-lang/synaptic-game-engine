TITLE: The Ink Fades
TEXT: Silence settles over the high galleried vaults like dry grey ash upon a neglected table. The heavy copper vats in the scriptoriums run sluggish and cold, their black liquor thickening into a useless sludge that refuses to take the nib. Across the Reading Rooms, scholars stare down at blank vellum sheets where yesterday's debt registers and birth-right codicils once glared in sharp iron-gall. The ink has simply lost its temper, lifting its pigment from the rag-paper and drifting upward in microscopic black motes to settle against the vaulted stone ceilings. Nothing remains upon the page save the dull indentation of the bone-folder and the faint, sweet smell of linseed oil and flax. Even the marginal notes, those malicious little whispers that used to carp at your margins, have grown toothless and grey. They flake away at a touch, leaving margins as white and smooth as an untouched drift of salt.

The heavy hereditary grudges that divided the guilds for three hundred years have dissolved overnight into thin air, leaving butchers and bakers to look across their stalls with polite, unremembering bewilderment. TITLE: The Empty Shelf

You lay your leather sorting apron across the edge of the high oak counter for the very last time. The air smells of cool limestone and old paper that has forgotten the touch of human thumbs. There are no fines to record, no overdue slips to harangue the students with, and no lost volumes waiting in the misfiled wing. You take your brass lamp from its peg, turn down the wick until the tiny blue bead of flame dies into a thread of grey smoke, and leave the great hall to the quiet administration of the dark.

Every book in the Underlibrary now sits at a precise ninety-degree angle to its neighbour, indexed, cross-referenced, and bound in stiff black buckram that admits no argument. The whispering marginalia have been systematically scraped from every leaf with razor-sharp steel erasers, leaving the margins as sterile and uniform as a freshly raked gravel walk. Rival scholars sit at their assigned carrels under green-shaded lamps, forbidden to speak, turning pages only when the master clock strikes the hour with a dull, pneumatic thud.

The lending clerk issues volumes with a rubber stamp that clicks like a falling guillotine, while the zealots of the Burners are safely bolted into the coal-cellars with their damp firewood and unlit torches. Nothing moves in the deep annexes unless a rule permits it, and no shelf shifts its position by so much as a hair's breadth from dawn to dusk. The air is entirely devoid of mystery; every shadow has been measured, docketed, and filed under its proper heading in the registry of approved silences.

TITLE: The Final Shelf-Mark
TEXT: The heavy iron latch falls home with a dry, satisfying click that seals the catalogued centuries into their permanent boxes. There are no more doors left to open and no more wandering corridors to trick the unwary page. You place your brass quill-knife in the velvet-lined drawer and turn the key twice, sliding the heavy bolt into its groove. The library sleeps at last beneath a mountain of stone and ink, perfect, immobile, and dead. You turn your back on the stacks and walk out into the quiet city, carrying nothing with you save the cold taste of rules and the immaculate, terrifying peace of a world that has been put entirely in its place.
