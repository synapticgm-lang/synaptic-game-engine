TITLE: The Last Tide
TEXT: Orsolya Venn was right about the weight of water, though she did not live to see the grey silt settle over her lighthouse door. You leave the salt-crusted helm of the Meridian Gear locked in its final, unyielding turn, watching from the high stone gallery as the first true rollers heave themselves back against the shingle of Gullmouth. The sea returns not with a roar, but with a heavy, sliding sigh, reclaiming the drowned alleys and the whale-bone shambles until the cliff-town floats once more upon its own narrow shadow. Down in the damp dark of the stairwell, the automaton-archivist folds its brass fingers and goes silent for good, its ink long dried in the well. There are no more charts to draw, no more secrets hidden in the dry salt-pans where the world used to end. You walk back down the cliff path through a mist that tastes of cold weed and old iron, your boots sinking into familiar mud. The tide clock in the tavern square has stopped its frantic ticking, its copper hand pointing straight up at the noon that never fails. Gullmouth is rich, wet, and safe behind its breakwaters, and the horizon is once more just an empty, grey line where the sky meets a flat, indifferent water.
